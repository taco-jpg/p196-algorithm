Even-base extension of the sharp four-step carry-class theorem

STATUS. Independent algebraic verification of the proposed generalization.
This extension to arbitrary bases is NOT formalized in the decimal Lean
artifact. The finite checks in the companion script validate calculations;
the arbitrary-base proof is the argument below.

THEOREM. For every even base b>=10 and every positive base-b integer a,
there is a positive integer n with the same base-b width and complete first
carry word as a such that T_b^k(n) is palindromic for some1<=k<=4. The
uniform bound4 is optimal, including if initial-time palindromes are allowed.

NORMALIZATION AND REALIZATION.
For mirrored outgoing carries u_i=c_(i+1),v_i=c_(L-i), put qhat_i equal to
b-1 when u_i!=v_i, b when both are1, and ell when both are0; take ell2
without overflow and ell0 with overflow. A pair sum belowb-1 forces carry0;
a pair sum at leastb forces1; onlyb-1 can yield unequal reflected carries.
Thus the complete carry word is unchanged. The new q is symmetric with even
center, since b is even; its outer sum is positive. Split each pair into
base-b digits and split an odd center in half to obtain a positive,
same-width integer.

NO-OVERFLOW CASE.
The next pair sums are at most6 in the low/low carry case, at most2 in the
high/high case, and exactlyb-1 in the unequal case. All are <=b-1, so the
following addition produces a palindrome. This part needs onlyb>=8 and
an even normalized center.

OVERFLOW CASE: PROTECTED RUNS.
Let P_d contain words whose nonneutral symbols lie in{0,...,d,b}, and whose
every maximal run of neutral symbols b-1 has endpoints0 andb in either
order. Words are extended by zeros outside their support. The overflowing
normalized q lies inP_0 because unequal directional carries on a neutral
run require opposite reset types at its two ends.

Let O,N be the overflow and non-overflow pair-sum updates. On adjacent
nonneutral symbols x,y, the overflow output is rho(x)+rho(y), where
rho(x)=x for x<b-1 andrho(b)=1. Protected runs satisfy the exact local rules
  0,(b-1)^r,b -> b,(b-1)^(r-1),0,
  b,(b-1)^r,0 -> 0,(b-1)^(r-1),b,  r>=1.
Consequently O(P_0) is contained inP_2 and O(P_2) inP_4. No ordinary pair
creates a new neutral symbol because4<b-1. A symbol4 inO(P_2) comes only
from adjacent symbols2,2; neither can neighbor a neutral run, whose low
endpoint must be0. Therefore both neighbors of this output4 are low.

Under N, a protected neutral run remainsb-1, a b yields at most2, and an
ordinary x yields2x plus its two incoming carries. N(P_2) is entirely low.
In N(O(P_2)), ordinary x<=3 gives at most8; an x=4 has low neighbors,
so its output is exactly8. All these values are <=b-1 becauseb>=10.

The outer-symbol schedules are forced:
  outerb -> outer1: overflow, then non-overflow;
  outerb-1 with overflow -> outerb -> outer1: overflow, overflow, non-overflow.
Thus qhat reaches an entirely low pair-sum word in two or three operations;
one further operation is palindromic. The width-one case is b/2 ->10_b ->11_b.

SHARPNESS.
Use the complete carry word(c0,...,c5)=(0,0,0,1,1,1). Every representative
must have q=(b-1,b-1,b+2r,b-1,b-1), where0<=r<=b/2-1. Its first three
iterates have the following digits, all written LEAST significant first:
  T^1: [b-1,b-1,2r,0,0,1],
  T^2: [0,0,2r+1,2r,b-1,0,1],
  T^3: [1,0,2r,(4r+1) mod b,2r+floor((4r+1)/b),1,1].
The first is nonpalindromic because its outside digits differ; the second
has outside digits0,1; the third has second-outside digits0,1. The original
input cannot be palindromic because its outer pair sum b-1 is odd.
For r=0, choose the ordinary most-significant-first numeral
  n=((b-1)(b-1)(b/2)00)_b.
Its fourth iterate is(2102012)_b, a palindrome. This proves optimality.

CHECKS.
resume_even_base_normalization_check.py checked30,000 randomly generated
original integers,5,568 normalized overflowing carry words (all retained
bit patterns through width12, across the selected bases), and1,756 sharpness
parameters. The selected even bases range from10 through1,000,000. All passed.
These are reproducible finite checks, not the proof of the universal claim.
