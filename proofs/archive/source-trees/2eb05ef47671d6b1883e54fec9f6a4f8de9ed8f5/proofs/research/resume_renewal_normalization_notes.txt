Overflow carry normalization: an arbitrary-length finite certificate

STATUS. An auxiliary theorem, not a decimal Lychrel existence proof.
No novelty claim is made. The mathematical reduction and exact finite-state
check are given below. The graph safety theorem is kernel-checked in
resume_renewal_certificate.lean. The complete numeric theorem and its
actual-input corollary are now kernel-checked in resume_renewal_theorem.lean,
using the generic bridge, two-branch bridge, numeric normalization, and
extraction modules. Only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound occur;
there is no sorry, custom axiom, native_decide, or remaining hypothesis gap.

THEOREM. Every complete carry word that occurs in one decimal reverse-and-add
operation on a positive integer also occurs for a positive integer reaching
a palindrome within at most four reverse-and-add operations.

The non-overflow case was already proved in novel_backward_carry.lean. The
new part concerns an overflowing carry word. Write c_0=0 and c_L=1, with
c_(i+1) the carry leaving position i, and let
  a_i=c_(i+1), b_i=c_(L-i).
Normalize the symmetric pair-sum word by
  qhat_i=0 if a_i=b_i=0;
  qhat_i=10 if a_i=b_i=1;
  qhat_i=9 if a_i != b_i.

This has the same carries as the original word. Values0 and10 force outgoing
carry0 and1. At unequal mirrored outgoing carries, the original symmetric
column sum must have been9, which copies its incoming carry; keeping9 retains
that behavior. Induction across columns proves equality of all carries.
The word qhat is symmetric; its odd center is0 or10; and its outer symbol is
9 or10 because c_L=1. It therefore has a positive canonical decimal realization
by the already formalized pair-sum realization theorem.

CLAIM A. If qhat_0=10, after two pair-sum transformations every symbol is at
most9. Hence the third numeric reverse-and-add result is a palindrome.
CLAIM B. If qhat_0=9, after three pair-sum transformations every symbol is at
most9. Hence the fourth numeric reverse-and-add result is a palindrome.
For length1 only qhat=(10) occurs, and5 ->10 ->11 settles it directly.

The overflow schedules in those claims are forced, not assumptions about a
candidate orbit: when outerq is10, the next outerq is1; when outerq is9 and
overflow occurs, the next outerq is10, followed by1. Outerq1 cannot overflow.
Thus schedules are(1,0) and(1,1,0), respectively.

EXACT INPUT AUTOMATON.
Read the folded pair sums from outside toward the center. State(a,b) stores
the carry entering the low column and leaving its mirrored high column.
Start at(0,1). The complete transition list is:
  if b=0: emit0 and move to(0,0) or(0,1);
  if b=1: emit10 and move to(1,0) or(1,1);
  if a!=b: emit9 and remain at(a,b).
At even length accept exactly when a=b. At odd length the central symbol is
10*b; this always has the required entering/leaving carries. These are exactly
the normalized words, by the normalization definition above. Restrict the
first pair to10 for ClaimA and9 for ClaimB.

EXACT OUTPUT TRANSDUCER.
At one time layer with overflow h, state(a,b,t) has low entering carry a,
high outgoing carry b, and delayed high output digit t when h=1. The initial
state is(0,h,1) for overflow and(0,0,-1) otherwise. On inputq, choose bn in{0,1}
with floor((q+bn)/10)=b. Put
  an=floor((q+a)/10), lo=(q+a)mod10, hi=(q+bn)mod10.
The next state is(an,bn,hi) for h=1 and(an,bn,-1) for h=0. Emit lo+t for
h=1, or lo+hi for h=0. At the center:
  even input: require a=b, then output center2t for h=1 or no center for h=0;
  odd input centerz: require floor((z+a)/10)=b; if h=0 output center
  2*((z+a)mod10); if h=1 emit one final pair ((z+a)mod10)+t and no center.
This is the existing, independently derived exact folded reverse-and-add
transducer. Composing layers is ordinary streaming composition; a final extra
pair is sent through the remaining layers before their center conditions.

FINITE CERTIFICATE.
The composed graph stores the input automaton state, each layer's transducer
state, and a Boolean flag recording whether a final output pair exceeded9.
A terminal state is unsafe if the flag is true or the final center exceeds9.

Breadth-first closure gives:
  first10, schedule(1,0): 37 reachable states,89 edges,11 accepted center
  completions,0 unsafe completions;
  first9, schedule(1,1,0):159 reachable states,391 edges,25 accepted center
  completions,0 unsafe completions.
There is no word-length or integer-size cutoff. Every successor of every
reachable state is visited. Induction on the number of read pairs therefore
covers every finite normalized input word. The zero unsafe completions prove
ClaimsA andB, and hence the theorem.

LIMITATION AND RELEVANCE.
This cannot disprove a Lychrel candidate: its q values change during
normalization, even though the entire first carry word remains identical.
It proves that any successful positive invariant must retain information
beyond a single complete carry word. Such information includes the excess
q-qhat, more than one time layer, or an equivalent numerical coordinate.
