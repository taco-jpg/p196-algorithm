TARGETED PRIOR-ART CHECK: UNBOUNDED DIGIT SUM IN REVERSE-AND-ADD
Date: 2026-10-14

Scope
-----
The manuscript continuation/sparse_orbits_theorem.md claims that, for each
fixed integer base b >= 2 and each positive starting integer, the ordinary
reverse-and-add orbit, continued after palindromes, has unbounded base-b
digit sum. The quantitative decimal claim concerns the running maximum,
not a pointwise lower bound: max_{0<=t<=N} S(n_t) = Omega(sqrt(N)) for a fixed
starting integer. The distinctive argument bounds zero runs in the symmetric
pair-sum word by either an inherited zero run or a sufficiently expensive
run of coefficients equal to b-1.

Provisional outcome
-------------------
No source actually inspected in this targeted check was found to state this
universal digit-sum theorem, the stated running-maximum bound, or the exact
zero-run inheritance inequalities. This is a search outcome, NOT a finding
of novelty. The pair-sum representation, carry analysis, and growing-block
reverse-and-add constructions have substantial prior art. The report below
distinguishes those established ingredients from the particular conclusion
being sought. Important publication-version and older-source gaps remain.

PRIMARY SOURCES CHECKED

1. Charles W. Trigg, Versum sequences in the binary system,
   Pacific Journal of Mathematics 47(1) (1973), 263-275.
   Publisher PDF:
   https://msp.org/pjm/1973/47-1/pjm-v47-n1-p25-p.pdf
   Correction:
   https://msp.org/pjm/1973/49-2/pjm-v49-n2-p35-p.pdf

   The full paper was inspected. It expressly follows continuing binary
   versum orbits, including after palindromes. Pages 266-273 give families
   of four-step block cycles, in which the lengths of specified blocks grow.
   The summary and conjectures (pp. 273-274) concern binary orbit mergers,
   palindrome occurrences, and recursive digit patterns. No general
   digit-sum theorem or mass-controlled maximum-zero-run argument was found.
   The correction changes an introductory reference and the misprinted
   first page number; it does not add such a theorem.

   Relevance: clear older precedent for finite words with growing zero and
   nonzero blocks. A fixed number of RUNS is not a fixed number of NONZERO
   DIGITS. These families do not contradict the present bounded-digit-sum
   obstruction.

2. Vincent Prosper and Sebastien Veigneau, On the palindromic reversal
   process, Calcolo 38 (2001), 129-140.
   Publisher metadata and abstract:
   https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s100920170002
   DOI: 10.1007/s100920170002
   Public preprint actually read:
   https://www.p196.org/files/revpal.pdf

   IMPORTANT VERSION DISTINCTION: the downloaded 13-page PDF has a title-page
   date of June 18, 1997. Its complete pages were inspected visually because
   the embedded text extraction is corrupted. The publisher confirms the
   2001 article, but its final full text was not obtained.

   In the preprint, Section 2 uses a stop-at-palindrome convention. Section
   3 studies transformations preserving future iterates. Section 4.1, p. 6,
   explicitly replaces opposite digits by their average; its pseudopalindrome
   is exactly half of the manuscript's pair-sum word. Proposition 4.7 relates
   its value to reverse-and-add. Appendix B, pp. 11-12, classifies inputs
   yielding a palindrome despite carries, with a separate base-2 remark.
   No unbounded digit-sum or zero-gap inheritance result was found in this
   preprint. This is a direct attribution source for the symmetric
   digit-pair representation, but the final publication should still be
   checked before a novelty statement.

3. Klaus Brockhaus, On the 'Reverse and Add!' algorithm in base 2,
   author note, June 4, 2001, hosted by OEIS:
   https://oeis.org/A058042/a058042.txt

   The complete note was read. It defines the indefinitely continued process
   and proves a four-family transition cycle containing the orbit of 22.
   Its proposition is about avoiding palindromes in this particular family,
   not universal digit-sum growth. Directly counting ones in its displayed
   families gives unbounded and indeed linearly growing digit sums through
   the cycle. This count is an inference from the explicit formulas, not a
   separate universal theorem asserted by Brockhaus.

4. Kevin Brown, Self-Similar Reverse-Sum Sequences, author technical note:
   https://www.mathpages.com/home/kmath312/kmath312.htm
   Earlier preserved version:
   https://www.p196.org/html/papers2.html
   Related author note, Binary Reverse-Sum Automata:
   https://www.mathpages.com/home/kmath602/kmath602.htm

   The main note analyzes self-similar orbits with fixed strings separated
   by variable blocks of 0 and b-1, and derives conditions for the fixed
   inner and outer strings. It distinguishes finite decimal patterns that
   eventually fail from genuine indefinitely growing cycles in other bases.
   Thus separating blocks by zeros and complementary nonzero runs is old.
   No theorem excluding every bounded-digit-sum orbit, and no universal
   maximum-zero-run inequality, was found. Its four-step and six-step
   constructions retain growing nonzero blocks and do not supply a
   counterexample to the digit-sum claim. The related binary note discusses
   algebraic growth rates of regular examples and unresolved irregular
   behavior, rather than a universal digit-sum bound.

5. David Seal, sporadic self-similar reverse-sum constructions, original
   rec.puzzles posting (January 1996), preserved with attribution at:
   https://www.mathpages.com/home/dseal.htm

   The displayed families in bases 4, 11, 17, and 26 were inspected. Each
   listed cycle includes growing blocks of nonzero digits; the base-20
   example is mentioned but not transcribed. The visible cycles are not
   bounded-support examples. Their status as a transcribed author posting,
   not a refereed general theorem, should be retained in any citation.

6. Stephen Wolfram, A New Kind of Science (2002), p. 905, note on
   reversal-addition systems:
   https://www.wolframscience.com/nks/notes-4-2--reversal-addition-systems/

   This primary account states that binary width expands by at least one
   digit every two steps, describes commonly observed four-step regularity,
   and gives computational behavior for the orbit starting at 512. It also
   distinguishes ordinary canonical reversal from artificial rules retaining
   fixed width or padding a new zero at every step. No universal digit-sum
   or zero-gap theorem was found. Width growth alone is not digit-sum growth;
   the manuscript's extra zero-gap argument cannot be replaced by this
   statement without further reasoning.

7. Ralph E. Griswold, The Versum Problem -- Continued, October 1995,
   revised September 22, 1999, pp. 5-7 of the revised note:
   https://www2.cs.arizona.edu/icon/oddsends/versum/Versum02.pdf
   Author's complete twelve-note series:
   https://www2.cs.arizona.edu/icon/oddsends/versum/

   The relevant note was read; all twelve PDFs were also downloaded and
   text-searched for digit sums, boundedness, nonzero digits, zero runs,
   sparsity, and growth. The note reports observed digit frequencies in
   20,000 terms from 196 and distinguishes empirical regularity from proof.
   Its average-digit and palindrome-probability discussion is not a
   universal theorem. No hit establishing the target claim was found in
   the series. Keyword screening of a series is weaker evidence than a
   line-by-line proof audit of every note.

8. Yutaka Nishiyama, Numerical Palindromes and the 196 Problem,
   International Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics 80(3) (2012),
   375-384.
   Publisher PDF:
   https://ijpam.eu/contents/2012-80-3/9/9.pdf
   Author-hosted earlier version:
   https://yutaka-nishiyama.sakura.ne.jp/math2010kr/196.pdf

   The publisher PDF's discussion and references were inspected. Its scope
   is elementary decimal palindrome examples, known numerical searches,
   history, and heuristic rarity of palindromes. The numerical width-growth
   discussion is empirical and specific to the computed orbit. No target
   digit-sum theorem or zero-gap mechanism was found. This is useful
   historical context, not an adequate source for the new proof's key lemma.

9. David G. Radcliffe, On the binary reverse-and-add trajectory of 10110_2,
   author note, March 17, 2026:
   https://radcliffe.dev/pdf/binary-reverse-add-22.pdf
   https://radcliffe.dev/math/2026/03/17/binary-reverse-add-22.html

   The four-page proof gives explicit block formulas for the binary orbit
   of 22 and verifies its four-step recurrence. It is not a general digit-sum
   result. Counting its displayed formulas gives S(a(4k))=S(a(4k+1))=
   S(a(4k+2))=k+3 and S(a(4k+3))=k+4 for k>=1. This calculation is our
   inference from the formulas. In particular the classical long zero
   blocks in this orbit are accompanied by increasing numbers of ones.

10. Robert Styer, The Palindromic Conjecture and The Fibonacci Sequence,
    Villanova University manuscript, revised September 1986, 11 pages:
    https://www41.homepage.villanova.edu/robert.styer/PalindromePaper1986.pdf

    The complete scan was downloaded, OCR-read, and key pages visually
    checked. The web tool alone failed to fetch it; ordinary HTTPS succeeded.
    Section 2 defines symmetric remainder/shift arrays R_i=q_i mod b and
    S_i=floor(q_i/b), so it predates Prosper--Veigneau's equivalent encoding.
    Section 3 proves uniqueness of the pair at a fixed input length.
    Page 6 states at most 2+[log_2 b] pairs of any fixed length in an orbit,
    with an argument based on the smallest integer of that length. Thus a
    universal linear width-growth observation is also prior art.
    Section 5, Lemmas 2-3 (p. 8), gives adjacent-column restrictions and
    splitting at R_k=S_k=0 when studying a PALINDROMIC ONE-STEP OUTPUT.
    It does not state a dynamical zero-run inheritance inequality for all
    outputs or a digit-sum unboundedness theorem. The later theorems classify
    palindrome-producing pairs, including the binary Fibonacci count.

    Relevance: an especially close primary source for carry-based local
    arguments, and essential background attribution. Its zero separator
    lemma addresses a different property from the manuscript's mass bound.

STRONGER CLAIMS AND COUNTEREXAMPLES

* No primary source inspected here provides a counterexample to pointwise
  divergence S(n_t)->infinity. No such divergence theorem was found either.
  Its status should therefore remain "not established by this argument or
  this literature check", not "known false" or "known open" without an
  appropriate reference.
* The classical growing-block cycles do refute an overly broad assertion
  that no orbit can have a fixed number of digit RUNS or a finite block
  description. They do not refute unbounded nonzero-digit count or mass.
* Digit sum need not be monotone: 1099+9901=11000 drops the decimal digit
  sum from 19 to 2. This direct calculation is not a counterexample to a
  pointwise limit along one infinite orbit.
* Unbounded zero runs in the integer itself do not contradict the proof:
  its controlled quantity is zero runs in d_i+d_(L-1-i), under a hypothetical
  uniform digit-sum bound. A zero digit opposite a nonzero digit contributes
  positively to that pair-sum word.

SEARCH METHOD AND SAFE PUBLICATION WORDING

Queries included exact variants of reverse-and-add / reverse and add /
reversal-addition / versum with digit sum(s), unbounded, bounded, nonzero
digits, zero runs, and sparse; then citations and author-hosted material were
followed through Trigg, Prosper--Veigneau, Brockhaus, Brown/Seal, Wolfram,
Griswold, Nishiyama, and Styer. Both available search engines were used.
Generic school exercises, generated summaries, and unverified broad claims
were not used as mathematical evidence. Search indexing was demonstrably
weak for old PDFs, so citation-following and direct source inspection carry
more weight than empty search results.

Suggested cautious wording:
"We give an elementary zero-run proof of unbounded digit sums for every
positive reverse-and-add orbit in each fixed base. We make no claim of
priority. The symmetric digit-pair representation, width-growth observations,
and explicit growing-block examples have earlier precedents; this note isolates
the stated
zero-run inequalities and their bounded-mass consequence."

Before calling this a new theorem, obtain the final Prosper--Veigneau article
and the outstanding classical texts, and seek a specialist's bibliographic
check. That is separate from validating whether the proof is correct.
