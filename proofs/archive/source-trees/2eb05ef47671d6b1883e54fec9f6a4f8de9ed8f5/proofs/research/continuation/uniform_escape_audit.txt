Independent audit: uniform escape from a fixed digit-sum ceiling
================================================================

Verdict
-------
The existential uniform escape theorem is established by the compactness
mechanism below. For every integer base b>=2 and integer B>=1, there is
a finite integer F_b(B), independent of the positive starting number and
its initial width, such that
  for every n>0, some 0<=t<=F_b(B) has S_b(T_b^t(n))>B.
The argument is a paper proof, not a new Lean theorem. It proves existence
of a uniform finite bound; the explicit recurrence proposed separately
by sparse_orbits requires its own additional separation/coupling proof
and is not certified by this audit.

The proof can avoid the proposed split into bounded and unbounded
starting widths. A half-line map exactly extends ALL finite symmetric
pair-sum words. Compactness then takes place in a larger space of finite,
not necessarily symmetric profiles, where the same obstruction holds.

1. Exact half-line extension
---------------------------
Fix b>=2. Let D consist of profiles
  q:N->{0,...,2b-2}
with finite support and q_0>0. Put R=max{i:q_i>0}. Define the forward
carries, forward digits, backward carries, and backward digits by
  a_0=0,
  a_(i+1)=floor((q_i+a_i)/b),
  r_i=q_i+a_i-b a_(i+1),
  beta_i=floor((q_i+beta_(i+1))/b),
  u_i=q_i+beta_(i+1)-b beta_i.
The backward sequence is the unique one with beta_i=0 for i>R; it is
computed from right to left. All carries lie in {0,1} and all r_i,u_i
lie in {0,...,b-1}. In particular, r_(R+1)=a_(R+1) must be retained.
For i>R+1 all forward digits vanish, and for i>R all backward digits
vanish. Put h=beta_0 and define F:D->D by
  if h=0:  F(q)_i=r_i+u_i                    for every i>=0;
  if h=1:  F(q)_0=r_0+1,
           F(q)_i=r_i+u_(i-1)               for every i>=1.

The no-overflow boundary is r_0+u_0, NOT r_0+h. The latter expression
would be wrong in that branch. The displayed overflow boundary r_0+1
is correct.

This map preserves finite support and the coefficient range. At index
zero, the overflow value is at most b<=2b-2, including b=2; all other
values are sums of two digits. If h=0, then q_0+beta_1<b, so q_0<b,
a_1=0, and
  F(q)_0=2q_0+beta_1>=2q_0>0.
If h=1, F(q)_0=r_0+1>0. Thus the anchored positivity condition is
preserved as well.

For an actual positive base-b integer of width L, extend its symmetric
pair-sum word q_0,...,q_(L-1) by zeros. Reflection of the actual forward
carry sequence gives beta_i=a_(L-i) for 0<=i<=L. Both sides have the
same backward recursion and terminal value zero. Hence h=a_L. In the
h=0 branch, F agrees with the next pair-sum word of width L and is zero
beyond it. In the h=1 branch its value at i=L is
  r_L+u_(L-1)=1+r_0,
and the value at i=0 is the same, giving exactly the new word of width
L+1. All subsequent entries vanish. Therefore iterating F on a genuine
pair-sum word reproduces the actual reverse-and-add pair-sum orbit,
including every boundary column and every overflow.

2. Support cannot retreat and expands at overflow
-------------------------------------------------
For a general q in D, beta_(R+1)=0, so u_R=q_R mod b and
r_(R+1)=a_(R+1). Both output sequences vanish farther to the right.

If h=0 and a_(R+1)=1, then F(q)_(R+1)=1, so R'=R+1.
If h=0 and a_(R+1)=0, then 1<=q_R<=b-1, and u_R=q_R>0, so R'=R.
Thus, exactly,
  R'=R+a_(R+1)                             when h=0.

If h=1, then
  F(q)_(R+1)=a_(R+1)+(q_R mod b)>0.
Indeed, if its first summand vanishes, then 1<=q_R<=b-1 and its second
summand is q_R>0. There is no support after R+1. Therefore
  R'=R+1                                  when h=1.
These arguments include R=0 and base two.

3. Zero-gap propagation still applies without symmetry
-------------------------------------------------------
Let Z(q) be the maximum zero-run length between indices 0 and R, with
value zero when there is no zero. Although q is no longer assumed
symmetric, it still satisfies BOTH exact carry equations
  q_i+a_i=r_i+b a_(i+1),
  q_i+beta_(i+1)=u_i+b beta_i.
The simultaneous-zero proof uses only these equations, binary carries,
and nonnegative q. It does not use global symmetry. An interval of at
least two simultaneous zeros is consequently all 0 or all b-1.

The previous zero-gap proof applies with mass M(q)=sum_i q_i:
  Z(F(q))<=max(Z(q),1,floor(M(q)/(b-1)))                  if h=0,
  Z(F(q))<=max(max(Z(q)-1,0),2,floor(M(q)/(b-1))+1)      if h=1.
Here is the boundary check needed for this extension. By definition the
new first and last support entries are positive, so a new zero interval
[A,B] is internal. In either branch R'<=R+1, hence B<=R. In the h=0
branch its entire old comparison interval [A,B] lies in [0,R]. In the
h=1 branch the common core [A,B-1] and its extended old zero interval
[A-1,B] both lie in [0,R], because A>=1. Therefore neither the old
infinite zero tail nor an artificial endpoint is counted as an old
bounded internal gap. Every mass estimate also involves genuine old
support-range indices. The length-one and length-two exceptions remain
exactly the ones already handled in the original proof.

4. No infinite bounded-mass orbit exists in D
---------------------------------------------
Fix M>=1. Suppose q_t=F^t(q_0) has mass at most M at every time. Put
  K=max(Z(q_0),2,floor(M/(b-1))+1).
The preceding propagation inequalities give Z(q_t)<=K for every t.
There are at most M positive coefficients in q_t, and its two support
endpoints are positive. Hence its support span satisfies
  R_t+1 <= M+(M-1)K.

On the other hand, put r=ceil(log_2 b) and kappa=r+1. At every h=0
step the first coefficient doubles or more, while h=0 requires
q_0<=b-1. Since q_0>=1, kappa consecutive h=0 steps are impossible.
Every block of kappa steps therefore contains an h=1 step. Section 2
then gives
  R_t>=R_0+floor(t/kappa),
contradicting the fixed support-span ceiling. This rules out every
infinite bounded-mass half-line orbit, including nonsymmetric profiles.
The bound K here may depend on this orbit's initial profile. No uniform
initial zero-gap bound has been assumed.

5. Compactness and the tail-carry continuity issue
-------------------------------------------------
Let X_M be the profiles in D with mass at most M. It is a compact space
in the coordinatewise/product topology. More explicitly, inside the
product of the finite alphabet {0,...,2b-2}, it is the closed subset
specified by q_0>=1 and every finite partial sum being at most M.
Every such profile has at most M nonzero entries, hence finite support;
there are no extra infinite-support points in its closure. Arbitrarily
far mass may disappear in a limit, but the condition q_0>=1 prevents
the entire profile from disappearing.

Crucially, backward carries are continuous despite that possible loss
of distant mass. Let
  J=floor(M/(b-1)).
For any q in X_M and any i, the J+1 entries
  q_i,...,q_(i+J)
cannot all equal b-1, since that alone would cost more than M. Choose
an index j in this block with q_j != b-1. Then the map
  e |-> floor((q_j+e)/b), e in {0,1},
is constant: it is zero when q_j<=b-2 and one when q_j>=b. Therefore
beta_j is independent of the entire farther tail. Backward recursion
determines beta_i using only q_i,...,q_j. Thus beta_i has uniform finite
lookahead on X_M. Forward carries already depend only on finite prefixes.
A common bound is that coordinate F(q)_i depends only on
  q_0,...,q_(i+J+1)
for q in X_M, including the branch selector h=beta_0. Hence coordinatewise
convergence within X_M implies coordinatewise convergence of F(q).
This proves the required continuity without assuming continuity of total
mass or stability of the rightmost support index. Neither of those
latter quantities is continuous under mass escape, and neither is used.

6. Elementary diagonal contradiction giving a uniform bound
-----------------------------------------------------------
Suppose, contrary to uniform escape for fixed b,M, that for every N
there is q^(N) in D with
  mass(F^t(q^(N)))<=M for every 0<=t<=N.
All these bounded-prefix states have coefficients in a fixed finite
alphabet and a positive entry at index zero. A diagonal subsequence
N_j->infinity can therefore be chosen so that, for every fixed time t
and coordinate i, the coefficients F^t(q^(N_j))_i eventually stabilize.
Call their limits p_t(i). Each finite partial sum of p_t is at most M,
and p_t(0)>=1, so p_t belongs to X_M and has finite support.
For each fixed t and i, the finite-lookahead result in section 5 applies
to all sufficiently large j, because both times t and t+1 lie within
the N_j-prefix. It yields
  p_(t+1)(i)=F(p_t)(i).
Thus p_t is an infinite F orbit entirely in X_M, contradicting section 4.

Consequently there exists a finite integer G_b(M) such that every q in
D exceeds mass M at some time 0<=t<=G_b(M). The same bound trivially
covers profiles whose mass already exceeds M at time zero. Taking
M=2B and applying the exact embedding in section 1 gives
  F_b(B)=G_b(2B)
for the claimed ordinary digit-sum theorem.

The bounded-prefix quantifier is inclusive at both 0 and N. The limit
argument uses only fixed finite times before sending N_j to infinity.
The limiting initial gap bound is allowed to be much smaller than the
original gaps: its own finite value is enough for section 4. No mass
conservation in the limit, no preservation of symmetry in the limit, and
no uniform initial width bound are required.

7. Scope and limitations
-----------------------
This proves uniform finite escape for every fixed digit-sum ceiling,
not an explicit formula for G_b(M) or F_b(B). It implies that arbitrarily
long segments of a positive orbit cannot all remain below one fixed
ceiling. It still does not prove that the individual digit sums tend to
infinity: repeatedly crossing a ceiling is weaker than eventually
staying above it. No palindrome-attainment claim follows from this
argument alone.
