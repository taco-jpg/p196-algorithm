Exploratory stack route: exact prefix balance

Status: no closed pushdown language and no Lychrel witness were obtained.

Let q[0..L-1] be the symmetric pair-sum word, c[0]=0,
c[i+1]=floor((q[i]+c[i])/10), and h=c[L]. Put
Q[j]=sum(q[0..j-1]), and write Q'[j] for the prefix sums of
G(q), the next pair-sum word.

For no overflow (h=0), for every 1 <= j <= L,
Q'[j] = 2 Q[j]
        - 9 sum_{i=1}^{j-1} (c[i]+c[L-i])
        - 10 c[j] + c[L-j].

For overflow (h=1), for every 1 <= j <= L,
Q'[j] = Q[j]+Q[j-1]
        - 9 sum_{i=1}^{j-1} (c[i]+c[L-i+1])
        - 10 c[j] + c[L-j+1].

Proof: write output digits d[i]=q[i]+c[i]-10c[i+1].
For h=0, sum d[i]+d[L-1-i] over i<j and telescope.
For h=1, append q[L]=0 and c[L+1]=0, so d[L]=c[L]=1;
then sum d[i]+d[L-i]. Symmetry of q gives respectively
2Q[j] and Q[j]+Q[j-1]. The carry terms telescope as displayed.

These identities expose the obstacle to a naive parenthesis stack:
the interior paired-carry term changes an unbounded number of units,
whereas the boundary term is bounded. Merely preserving a positive
prefix excursion does not follow from local boundary estimates.

Concrete candidate tested: folded q-symbols have charge q-9, their
prefix sums are nonnegative, final sum is zero, with a nontrivial
positive excursion. The word (10,8) satisfies this condition.
It is represented by decimal n=1719. But
1719+9171=10890, whose folded word is (1,9), center16;
the first new prefix charge is -8. Thus that language is not closed.

More generally, an invariant requiring the outer q-symbol to be
high (q[0]>=10) cannot be closed even for one step: the final carry
is forced to be one by symmetry, and the next outer q is q[0]-9,
which is at most nine. A useful pushdown invariant therefore needs
a temporal phase, a moving root, or a less restrictive prefix rule.
This statement does not rule out pushdown invariants in general.

The executable checks both exact prefix identities on all 75,447
canonical folded words with at most three outer pairs and any valid
center. This finite validation supplements, and does not replace,
the algebraic proof above. No novelty claim is made.
