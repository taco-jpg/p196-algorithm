EXACT INVERSE STRUCTURE OF REVERSE-AND-ADD
Research note; decimal unless a base is explicitly specified.
This note does not prove that a Lychrel number exists.

Definitions
-----------
A canonical pair-sum word q has length L>0, q_i in {0,...,18},
q_i=q_(L-1-i), q_0>0, and an even central coefficient when L is odd.
Every such word is realized by a positive L-digit integer. Write

  S(q)=sum_i q_i 10^i,
  G(q)=the symmetric pair-sum word of the canonical digits of S(q).

These are different maps. S(q) is the numerical reverse-and-add value;
G(q) is the autonomous state transition on pair-sum words.

1. Fixed-width injectivity of S (valid in every base b>=2)
-------------------------------------------------------
Suppose q,r are symmetric words of the same length, with coefficients
between 0 and 2b-2, and S(q)=S(r). Their canonical output digits and final
carries coincide. If e_i is the difference of their carries, then

  e_0=e_L=0, e_i in {-1,0,1},
  q_i-r_i = b e_(i+1)-e_i.

By symmetry,

  b(e_(i+1)-e_(L-i)) = e_i-e_(L-1-i).

An outside-in induction proves every e_i zero: if e_i=e_(L-i)=0,
then b e_(i+1)=-e_(L-1-i), and the right side has absolute value at most
one. Since b>=2, both new terms vanish. Consequently q=r.

For a positive target of width M, a canonical predecessor word can have
only width M or M-1. Thus S has at most two canonical q preimages.

2. Necessary conditions for ambiguity between the two widths
------------------------------------------------------------
In a decimal cross-width collision, the shorter word overflows and the
longer does not. The common output has leading digit 1. For the longer
word, its outer coefficient is positive, equals the units output digit,
and is at most the leading output digit. Hence the output units digit
and the longer outer coefficient are both 1. For the shorter word the
outer coefficient is consequently 11. The common output is divisible
by 11 because one of the two symmetric input lengths is even.

The common output need not be palindromic. For example,

  S(11,9,7,7,9,11) = S(1,0,18,16,18,0,1) = 1197801.

So an argument that only palindromic output values have two q preimages
would be false.

3. Which decimal palindromes are numerical images?
--------------------------------------------------
A positive decimal palindrome P has a positive reverse-and-add
predecessor if and only if P has even width or an even central digit.
For width one, the possibilities are exactly 2,4,6,8.

Proof: Without overflow, a palindromic output forces every carry to be
zero, so q is the digit word of P. It is realizable exactly when its
center is even (if present). With overflow, the exact palindrome
criterion is q_0=11 and q_i in {0,11}; an odd center is necessarily 0.
Write q=11c for a symmetric binary word c with outer coefficient 1.
Then S(q)=11 C(10), whose decimal digits are c_i+c_(i-1), using zero
outside the word. These digits are in {0,1,2}, and an odd central digit
is twice a binary coefficient. Hence this output also has the
same-width no-overflow q preimage just described.

For target width M=2m there are 9*10^(m-1) admissible palindrome targets.
For M=2m+1>=3 there are 45*10^(m-1). The extra, one-shorter overflow
preimage occurs at exactly 2^(floor((M-1)/2)-1) targets for M>=3;
there are no such extra targets at widths 1 or 2.

4. Exponentially many preimages for G: overflow
----------------------------------------------
Choose x_1,...,x_m independently in {1,10}. The 2^m distinct words

  q=(10,x_1,...,x_m,x_m,...,x_1,10)

all have the same G-image: outer coefficients 1 and every interior
coefficient 2. Indeed, there are no neutral coefficients 9, and in
the overflow transition each interior coefficient is

  rho(q_i)+rho(q_(i-1)),  rho(x)=x-9*[x>=10].

Both rho(1) and rho(10) are 1. The outer coefficient becomes 10-9=1.

5. Exponentially many preimages for G: no overflow
-------------------------------------------------
Set

  A=(1,9,10,0,10,9,1),
  B=(1,14,9,10,9,14,1),
  D=(3,9,0,2,0,9,3).

Both A and B are palindromic words whose carry starts and ends at zero.
Their output digit blocks are respectively

  (1,9,0,1,0,0,2),
  (1,4,0,1,0,5,2).

Each digit block plus its reversal equals D. Concatenate m independently
chosen A/B blocks followed by the same block list in reverse order.
The resulting 2^m distinct canonical symmetric words all have G-image
D repeated 2m times, and none overflows. Carry resets at block boundaries
justify applying the block calculation to the whole word.

These collisions need not happen immediately before a palindrome:
insert the central block C=(1,18,1). Its output digit block is (1,8,2),
whose pair-sum block is E=(3,16,3). Then all 2^m images are

  D repeated m times, E, D repeated m times.

This has a coefficient 16 and is not one of the overflow palindrome
patterns either. More generally a central block (1,18^k,1), k>=2,
maps to (3,17,18^(k-2),17,3), retaining an arbitrarily long high core.

6. Exact inverse transducer for a fixed-width numerical target
-------------------------------------------------------------
Scan a target digit word from both ends. At an input-pair location let
a be the already known low-side incoming carry and b the known
high-side outgoing carry. For output digits u,v, respectively, solve

  q+a=u+10 a',
  q+b'=v+10 b,

with q in {0,...,18} and a',b' in {0,1}. Equivalently

  u-v = a-b' + 10(b-a').

There is at most one solution: subtracting two candidates gives
10*Delta(a')=-Delta(b'), forcing both zero. The next state is (a',b').
Start at (0,h), where h is the candidate overflow bit. With no overflow
use all target digits; with overflow require leading digit 1 and remove
it before the scan. At an even center require the two carries equal;
at an odd center the sole q is output_center+10*b-a and must be even.

This gives a four-state exact membership test for the fixed-width
image, but composing predecessor steps involves digit-splitting choices
and therefore does not retain the two-predecessor bound.

Limitation relevant to the existence problem
--------------------------------------------
The at-most-two theorem concerns S, while iteration uses G. Sections 4
and 5 disprove an attempted bound of 2^t on a depth-t inverse tree of
pair-sum states. Counting a terminating basin still needs a uniform
bound on all possible terminal times. No such bound or positive
nonterminating invariant is supplied here.
