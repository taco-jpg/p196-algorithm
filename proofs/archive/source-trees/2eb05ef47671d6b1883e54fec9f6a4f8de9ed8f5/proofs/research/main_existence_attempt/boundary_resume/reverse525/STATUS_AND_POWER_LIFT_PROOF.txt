Why bisimulation needs powers
-----------------------------
A labeled forward bisimulation quotient has a block map p from old
vertices onto quotient vertices. For every old vertex u, the set

  {(label, p(v)) : u -(label)-> v}

must equal the quotient outgoing set at p(u). This is checked for EVERY
vertex, not just for one representative. It ensures that every quotient
edge can be realized from every member of its source block.

Projecting an old closed walk gives a quotient closed walk with the same
word. In the other direction a quotient closed walk with word w can be
followed repeatedly from any old vertex in its starting block. Choose a
fixed matching old edge whenever one is needed. At each complete traversal
of w, record the old vertex at temporal phase zero. There are finitely many
vertices; eventually one repeats. Discard the finite initial segment before
that repetition. The resulting old closed walk has word w^k for some k>=1.

Therefore quotienting preserves cyclic languages UP TO POSITIVE POWERS,
not at each fixed length. The six-cycle labeled ABCABC can quotient to an
ABC three-cycle, so treating the quotient as fixed-period exact would be
wrong. The witness lifting explicitly restores the necessary power.

Backward bisimulation applies the same construction to the graph with all
edges reversed. Its local condition compares incoming labeled edges.
Reverse the quotient closed walk, lift it in the reversed old graph as
above, and reverse the lifted walk back. Its word is again w^k. Thus the
same power semantics holds in either direction and under their composition.

Compatibility with spatial preimages
------------------------------------
For a cyclic language L, write Pow(L)={w: w^k belongs to L for some k>=1}.
The quotient argument proves equality of Pow(L) before and after quotienting.
The spatial column relation is equivariant under repeating temporal words:
if u maps to v with a particular column, then u^k maps to v^k with that
column repeated k times.

Consequently, if Pow(L1)=Pow(L2), then

  Pow(Pre(L1)) = Pow(Pre(L2)).

For example, u in Pre(L1) gives u->v with v in L1. Some power v^k belongs
to L2, so repeating the column gives u^k in Pre(L2); hence u belongs to
Pow(Pre(L2)). Repeating this argument for powers of u gives one inclusion;
interchanging L1 and L2 gives the other. This proves the all-period
semantics inductively through every quotient and spatial layer.

The exterior root is the constant word in letter41=(0,1,1,7). A quotient
root cycle would lift to another constant root word of a possibly larger
period. Thus compression preserves existence of some-period root exterior
and cannot hide a positive case. It does not authorize any fixed-period
claim from a compressed cycle.
