Period-24 decimal boundary search: phase reduction and exact spatial reachability

Status: no decimal Lychrel number has been proved. No positive family was found.
No novelty claim is made. This note concerns one particular family construction.

1. Exact phase reduction

Let B_t be the period-three infinite mirrored-sum bulk at time t, with overflow
schedule 1,0,1,0,... . The local bulk update is implemented by bulk_step.
The exterior is constrained to the eight-step three-pair clock in
novel_encoding_phase24_boundary.py. A candidate has an exterior prefix of at
most K pairs at every phase, followed by B_t repeated indefinitely, and returns
to its initial exterior after 24 steps.

Advancing a candidate by eight time steps preserves the exterior clock, period
24, and the same maximum exterior width K. It replaces the initial bulk B by
F(B), its eighth bulk iterate. Each eligible bulk has an F-orbit of length three.
Therefore existence for one bulk in an orbit is equivalent to existence for each
of the other two, at the SAME maximum width K. This is an exact symmetry, not an
empirical similarity between candidate words.

The thirty eligible bulks form these ten orbits:

(0,0,18) -> (0,18,0) -> (18,0,0)
(0,9,18) -> (9,18,0) -> (18,0,9)
(0,18,9) -> (18,9,0) -> (9,0,18)
(0,18,18) -> (18,18,0) -> (18,0,18)
(2,2,14) -> (14,8,14) -> (2,8,8)
(2,14,2) -> (8,14,14) -> (8,8,2)
(4,4,10) -> (10,16,10) -> (4,16,16)
(4,10,4) -> (16,10,10) -> (16,16,4)
(8,2,8) -> (14,2,2) -> (14,14,8)
(10,4,4) -> (10,10,16) -> (16,4,16)

Adding spatial rotation groups these into six classes, but rotation absorbs one
or two bulk letters into the prefix and may increase K. That coarser reduction
was NOT used to infer the fixed-width SAT results.

2. Completed previously unresolved width-16 search

CaDiCaL returned UNSAT for one representative in every one of the ten temporal
orbits, taking 1.168 to 11.401 seconds per instance in these runs. Thus all thirty
previously UNKNOWN width-16 instances are now excluded in this ansatz. The
aggregate evidence is resume_phase24_complete_width16.json. The CNF, solver log,
and individual result for every representative remain beside that file.

A repeated-call Z3 DIMACS export issue was discovered: the p cnf header sometimes
understates the maximum literal ID. CaDiCaL correctly rejects these malformed
files with a parse error and exit 1. The corrected runner recomputes the maximum
literal and clause count before solving. Every representative included in the
aggregate was audited for valid header bounds and clause counts and had CaDiCaL
exit 20 with s UNSATISFIABLE. A rejected file is not included as UNSAT evidence.

The older single check of bulk (2,8,8) at width 16 was separately audited: its
header is p cnf 13653 59603, maximum literal is 13653, actual clause count is
59603, and the solver reports UNSAT with exit 20. It was unaffected by the bug.

These are ordinary SAT solver results. No independently checked UNSAT proof
certificate was generated. The findings do not exclude a width-17 interface,
another period, another bulk, or a Lychrel number.

Reproduction (requires z3-solver and a CaDiCaL executable):

CADICAL=/absolute/path/to/cadical python3 resume_phase24_sat.py
python3 resume_phase24_sat.py --collect

3. Exact unbounded-width formulation

The exterior spatial automaton in continuation_theory_multigrowth.py has twelve
temporal segments, each of length two, ending with one overflow. Its state is
three arrays (L_j,A_j,B_j), j=0,...,11: a leading digit and two two-bit carry
masks per segment. Packing each entry into 8 bits yields a 96-bit state. A spatial
transition guesses the right digit and the 24 inward leading carry bits, performs
both exact decimal additions per segment, enforces the known carry masks, then
cyclically shifts the resulting leading digits. This is encoded as bit-vector
relation T(s,s') in resume_phase24_horn.py.

Each infinite period-three bulk supplies an exact three-cycle of spatial states.
resume_phase24_targets.py constructs these states using the bulk's left and
right carry streams. All 90 target-state transitions (three per eligible bulk)
were checked against the existing explicit spatial transition enumerator. The
packed relation was also compared with complete explicit successor sets on 24
small states at one and two temporal segments, with exact agreement.

Unbounded exterior reachability is the least fixed point:

    R(s') if T(start,s') and the first trailing digit is 7;
    R(s') if R(s) and T(s,s').

Here start has all leading digits 1, all leading outgoing carry masks 2, and all
trailing incoming carry masks 0. A target query asks whether R contains one of
the 30 temporal rotations of the bulk spatial cycles. An UNSAT answer would
exclude arbitrary finite exterior width for these targets. A SAT answer would
provide a candidate path needing reconstruction and full-family validation.

Z3 Spacer returned UNKNOWN (canceled at the explicit 55-second limit). Thus this
run supplies no arbitrary-width impossibility result and no positive witness.

Reproduction:

python3 resume_phase24_targets.py
python3 resume_phase24_horn.py --seconds 55

The files do not contain a proof of decimal Lychrel existence, of the 196
conjecture, or of their negations. The exact finite-state formulation is the
reusable output; the original requested theorem remains unresolved.
