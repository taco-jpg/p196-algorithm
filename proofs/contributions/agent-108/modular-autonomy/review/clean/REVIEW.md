## CounterexampleBound mathematical review

The definitions quantify over every pair n,m and every two digit functions d,e
satisfying `Canonical n d L` and `Canonical m e L` at the **same exact width L**.
There is no eventual-width assumption hidden in either definition.

The fixed-width classification explicitly assumes `0 < M`, `3 ≤ L`, and
`M + 10 < 10 ^ (L - 1)`. These are sufficient bounds; the result does not claim
that this threshold is minimal.

The leading-digit witnesses are multiples of M strictly below 2P and 3P,
respectively, where P is `10 ^ (L - 1)`. Their leading digits 1 and 2 distinguish
reversal modulo 2 and modulo 5, excluding those divisors of M under determinism.
The perturbation witnesses P+9, P+10, P+9+M, and P+10+M all retain exact width L
under the stated strict bound. The nonzero units residue of M ensures that the
second increment has no units carry, while the first increment has the explicit
single-nine reversal difference. The difference comparison forces
`M ∣ 99 * 10 ^ (L - 2)`; coprimality with 10 cancels the power and yields `M ∣ 99`.

The reverse-and-add equivalence cancels the input residue only after input residues
are assumed equal. The counterexample theorem negates this fixed-width universal
property, yielding witnesses at **each supplied L satisfying the bounds**, not
merely arbitrarily large unspecified widths. Distinct step residues also force the
two integers to be distinct, although an explicit inequality is unnecessary.
The final existential extraction uses ordinary classical logic, captured by the
allowed standard axiom audit. No quantifier or arithmetic-bound discrepancy was found.
