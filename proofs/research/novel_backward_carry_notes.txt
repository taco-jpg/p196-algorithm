Carry normalization and a limit of backward-rigidity arguments

Status: this proves an obstruction to one class of invariants. It does not
prove or disprove decimal Lychrel existence or the 196 conjecture. No novelty
claim is made. The theorem below is kernel-checked in novel_backward_carry.lean.

THEOREM. Every realizable complete non-overflow carry word of positive length
is also the complete carry word of a positive decimal integer whose second
reverse-and-add iterate is a palindrome.

Here q_i=d_i+d_(L-1-i), the q word is symmetric, incoming carry is c_0=0,
c_(i+1)=floor((q_i+c_i)/10), and non-overflow means c_L=0.

Proof. Write a_i=c_(i+1) and b_i=c_(L-i), the outgoing carries at mirrored
positions. Define a new symmetric word by

    qhat_i = 2   if a_i=b_i=0,
             10  if a_i=b_i=1,
             9   if a_i != b_i.

It is an admissible decimal pair-sum word: every symbol is between 0 and18,
its outer symbol is positive, and its central symbol (if there is one) is
either2 or10 and hence even. Thus it has a finite positive decimal realization.

The new word has exactly the old carries. Symbols2 and10 force respectively
carry0 and1 regardless of the incoming bit. If mirrored outgoing carries
differ in the old word, their common input q_i must have been9; values below9
force both carries0 and values above9 force both1. Thus the new9 copies the
old incoming carry unchanged as well. Induction across the columns proves
equality of the entire carry traces.

Because there is no overflow, the next mirrored sum at position i is the sum
of the two ordinary output digits. At qhat_i=2 this sum is at most6. At10 it
is at most2. At9 the two outgoing carries differ, and so the two incoming
carries also differ; the output digits are0 and9, whose sum is9. Consequently
every next mirrored sum is at most9. The following reverse-and-add operation
has no carries and produces a palindrome.

For the carry word of196 specifically, q=(7,18,7) normalizes to(2,10,2).
A corresponding concrete seed is250:

    250 -> 302 -> 505.

So the full carry word, including its global arrangement and length, cannot
by itself establish persistence for196. This does not rule out invariants
retaining q values, more than one time slice, or additional arithmetic data.

FORMAL VERIFICATION.
The standalone extension imports Lychrel196Research.lean and proves:
  norm_local
  normalized_preserves_carry
  normalized_symmetric
  normalized_admissible
  normalized_next_low

The last statement includes extraction of an actual finite positive integer,

ANOTHER FAILED IDEA, WITH AN UNBOUNDED COUNTERFAMILY.
View q symbols on the circle R/18Z and count nearest-neighbor phase winding
along the folded half-word. For every k>=1 use folded word (2,4,10,14)^k,2,
then reflect it about its final2. Principal increments are2,6,4,6 per block,
so the relative winding is k. There are no9 symbols and no overflow. The
next q symbols are only1,4,5,6,9; hence the second numeric iterate is a
palindrome. Thus arbitrarily large phase winding does not obstruct a
palindromic ancestor. At k=1:

    101519941 -> 251435042 -> 491969194.

The first theorem is formalized; this winding-family observation is an
arbitrary-length paper calculation checked by direct arithmetic at k=1.

UNPROVED EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATION, NOT USED IN ANY THEOREM.
For overflow carry words, choosing0/10/9 according to the same equal/unequal
carry cases was tested on all2^17 bit words of length17 (keeping only those
that actually occur), and on shorter lengths. Every such normalized state
reached a palindrome by four steps. No arbitrary-length proof is claimed.
The accompanying Python script is exploratory and is not a formal certificate.
