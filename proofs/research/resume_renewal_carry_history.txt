A bounded temporal carry history determines the complete pair-sum word

THEOREM (conditional only on the number of non-overflow steps).
Let two finite decimal reverse-and-add trajectories start with the same
number L of digits. Suppose their entire carry words agree at every time
before a common horizon t. If at least five of these steps do not overflow,
the trajectories have identical initial mirrored pair-sum words.

This determines the next integer exactly, since n+reverse(n)=sum q_i*10^i.
It need not determine the initial integer: different digit splittings can
have the same pair sums, for example196 and295.

PROOF.
For each trajectory form the symmetric pair-sum polynomial Q_t(x). Equality
of the complete carry words implies equal overflow bits, hence equal widths
at every subsequent time. The exact polynomial identities give, after
subtracting the two trajectories,
  D_(t+1)(x)=2 D_t(x),                 if that step does not overflow;
  D_(t+1)(x)=(1+x) D_t(x),             if that step overflows.
All additive carry terms cancel. Therefore
  D_t(x)=2^N (1+x)^H D_0(x),
where N and H are the numbers of non-overflow and overflow steps.

If D_0 is nonzero, let m be its least nonzero exponent and let a be its
coefficient there. Since (1+x)^H has constant coefficient1, the coefficient
at exponentm in D_t is2^N*a. It has absolute value at least2^N because a is
a nonzero integer. But every pair-sum coefficient is between0 and18, so every
coefficient of D_t lies between-18 and18. For N>=5 this is impossible since
32>18. Hence D_0=0.

COMBINING WITH THE OUTER-DIGIT GROWTH BOUND.
The independently derived potential bound2H<=t+3 gives
  N=t-H >= ceil((t-3)/2).
At t=12, N>=5. Thus twelve complete carry words, together with initial width,
already determine the initial q word. This twelve-step numerical bound relies
on the earlier outer-potential theorem; the coefficient argument itself only
needs the five-non-overflow hypothesis. Neither part proves a Lychrel witness.

WHY THIS MATTERS.
The one-step carry normalization theorem and this reconstruction theorem are
compatible. One carry word omits significant information; a short consecutive
time history can retain every pair sum. Therefore the normalization theorem
cannot rule out invariant approaches using several adjacent carry time slices.

SHARPENING TO ELEVEN CONSECUTIVE CARRY WORDS (FULLY FORMALIZED).
At a time where the carry words agree, the ordinary column equations give
  q_i+c_i=10c_(i+1)+d_i,  r_i+c_i=10c_(i+1)+e_i,
where d_i,e_i are decimal output digits. Consequently q_i-r_i=d_i-e_i lies
in[-9,9], a stronger bound than[-18,18]. Four earlier non-overflow steps
would make a nonzero first discrepancy at least16, which is impossible.
Apply the growth bound through ten steps:10<=2N+3 forcesN>=4. The eleven
carry words at times0,...,10 therefore determine the initial pair sums.
No optimality claim for eleven is made.

CONSTRUCTIVE RECONSTRUCTION.
The full polynomial recurrence isQ_t=M_tQ_0+R_t, withM_t=2^N(1+x)^H and
R_t computable from the supplied carry history. Recover coefficients ofQ_0
from lowest exponent upward. At positioni, subtract the already recovered
lower-coefficient contributions. The remaining unknown appears multiplied
by2^N. The allowed final coefficient interval has width18, or width9 when
the endpoint carry word is also given. A multiplier larger than that width
allows at most one integer coefficient. This is an exact sequential decoder,
not a claim that the carry history itself is easy to predict.
