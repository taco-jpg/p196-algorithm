EXACT COLUMN GRAPH FOR THE 24-PHASE ALTERNATING EXTERIOR

Scope
-----
The clock is N,O repeated twelve times, and the outermost pair-sum
coefficient is 8 at N phases and 17 at O phases. This is the prescribed
8/17 outer branch. The argument does not silently include other outer
branches. The desired exterior has fixed finite width and returns after
24 updates, attached to a specified periodic bulk with its cyclic carries.

State and one-column equations
------------------------------
For j=0,...,11, let Q be the coefficient at temporal phase 2j and R its
coefficient at phase 2j+1. At a spatial cut the state stores:

  a_j = incoming low carry at the N phase;
  b_j = incoming low carry at the O phase;
  c_j = outward high carry at the N phase;
  z_j = preceding O coefficient minus ten times its outward high carry.

Thus a,b,c are bits and z is in {-1,0,...,9}. The preceding O high digit
is z+e, where e is the O outward high carry at the current column. The
preceding carry equation is precisely 0 <= z+e <= 9; equivalently z=-1
forces e=1 and z=9 forces e=0.

Choose the N inward high carry d in {0,1}. Exact column arithmetic gives

  floor((Q+d)/10) = c,
  R = (Q+a) mod 10 + Q+d-10c.

Choose e in {0,1}. A future inward carry can match this O column exactly
when e=0 for R<9, e=1 for R>9, and either value is allowed for R=9.
The overflow comparison uses the preceding high digit, so

  Q_next = (R+b) mod 10 + z+e.

Require Q,R,Q_next in {0,...,18}, and require the twelve phase pairs to
close cyclically. The next spatial state is

  a' = floor((Q+a)/10),
  b' = floor((R+b)/10),
  c' = d,
  z' = R-10e.

The next column's z' constraint enforces the O carry equation left pending
here. Nothing is guessed without a corresponding next-column constraint.
The terminal periodic bulk supplies the final inward carries.

Initial and terminal states
---------------------------
The first exterior column is Q=8,R=17 at every phase pair. Its low incoming
carries are zero, and the outer high carry is the clock bit. Substitution
in the equations gives the state immediately after this column:

  a=(0)^12, b=(1)^12, c=(1)^12, z=(7)^12.

For a periodic bulk trace, write A_t[i] for its low incoming carry and
H_t[i] for its high outward carry. The matching terminal state is

  a_j=A_(2j)[0], b_j=A_(2j+1)[0], c_j=H_(2j)[0],
  z_j=q_(2j+1)[-1]-10 H_(2j+1)[-1].

The z equation matches the high digit from the final exterior column with
the high digit from the previous periodic bulk column. Consequently it
also enforces the first bulk output coefficient at overflow phases.
Nonoverflow matching uses c directly.

Completeness and finite bound
-----------------------------
Every valid exterior in this branch gives a path from the stated initial
state to the corresponding bulk state. Conversely, such a path supplies
all 24 coefficient rows and compatible carries. The implementation retains
the coefficient column for every path edge. A positive path is additionally
replayed with the separate ordinary integer column evaluator before being
used in a full finite family.

There are at most

  (2^3 * 11)^12 = 88^12 = 215671155821681003462656

states. If a target is reachable, remove repeated spatial states from a
path. Thus some exterior has width at most this number, including its first
column. This is an exact but impractically large completeness bound. The
three-million-state search caps are far below it and remain UNKNOWN for
unrestricted width.

Reverse one-column targets
--------------------------
Root's reverse generator supplies predecessor states and their final
coefficient column. Every retained reverse edge is checked by direct
forward equations. Searching their union is therefore a positive search
one column farther; a hit must append its stored suffix column and replay
the resulting complete exterior. No hit occurred in the saved runs.
