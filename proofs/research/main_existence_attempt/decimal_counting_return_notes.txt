DECIMAL COUNTING RETURN: THREE IMAGE LAYERS AND THE ALL-TIME GAP
Status: no decimal Lychrel existence proof, and no proof about 196.

1. An actual contraction after three incoming image layers
--------------------------------------------------------
Let G be the canonical symmetric pair-sum map used in research/README.md.
For y in {1,...,8} and m>=1 define Q_y(m) to consist of symmetric words
of width 2m, with outer coefficient y+9 and every remaining mirrored
pair independently chosen from {y,y+9}. Thus |Q_y(m)|=2^(m-1).
Every such word has the SAME overflowing G-image

  r_y(m)=(y, (2y) repeated (2m-1) times, y).

This is a sharp maximal one-step overflow fiber. The identity follows
from rho(x)=x-9*[x>=10], since rho(y)=rho(y+9)=y and no coefficient is 9.

The new finite certificate gives, for every y=1,...,8 and every m>=3,

 |Q_y(m) intersect Im(G^3)|
 <= 8 * 2^(m-1) * (511/512)^floor((m-3)/9).                 (1)

Here Im(G^3) means three actual prior canonical positive pair-sum words;
no safety requirement is imposed. Therefore the exponential rate per
mirrored pair of this restricted family is at most

  rho_3 = 2*(511/512)^(1/9) < 2  (approximately 1.999565595).

The numerical constant is deliberately coarse. The claim is UNIFORM IN
m and all eight constant-rho families, but it is NOT a bound for arbitrary
overflow targets or for arbitrary initial seeds.

This answers a specific unresolved checkpoint from the earlier counting
pass: two safe incoming layers preserve rate 2 in one such family;
three unrestricted incoming layers do force a strict rate loss in every
constant-rho maximal family, including arbitrary incoming overflow clocks.
Neither statement estimates the whole terminating basin.

2. Exact finite certificate and proof of (1)
------------------------------------------
In a single inverse layer, let h be its overflow bit, a the low incoming
carry, b the high outgoing carry, and t the delayed high digit if h=1.
For a candidate predecessor coefficient x and inward high carry b', put

 a'=floor((x+a)/10), lo=(x+a) mod 10,
 b =floor((x+b')/10), hi=(x+b') mod 10.

The target pair coefficient is lo+hi when h=0, and lo+t when h=1.
The next state is (a',b',-1) or (a',b',hi), respectively.
The initial state is (0,h,-1) or (0,h,1). These equations are the ordinary
column-addition equations; the delayed digit handles reflected alignment
at an overflow exactly.

Compose three such inverse layers, one for each backward clock
h=(h_1,h_2,h_3) in {0,1}^3. Read the target outer coefficient y+9 first,
and retain only paths whose three emitted outer coefficients are positive.
For subsequent coefficients read either y or y+9. Powerset determinization
counts accepted prefixes rather than counting different predecessor paths.
The empty subset is an absorbing rejecting state. Center acceptance is
intentionally omitted; the automata give necessary prefix conditions.

The certificate records all reachable determinized states and both outgoing
transitions for all 64 (clock,y) cases. There are 2,921 states in total.
Every nonempty reachable state has a recorded path of length at most 9
to the empty state. The largest shortest killing distances, by y, are

 y:       1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
 K_y:     5 7 9 9 9 9 7 5.

Thus from every nonempty state at least one of the 512 binary words of
length 9 kills the path. Because the empty state stays empty, from any
starting state the number of nonrejected binary words of length n is at most

  2^n * (511/512)^floor(n/9).

Actual ancestors may have widths 2m,2m-1,2m-2,2m-3. Even at the shortest
width they have m-2 full mirrored pairs. Hence the first m-2 pairs of q,
consisting of its outer pair and m-3 free pairs, necessarily traverse the
prefix automaton. The final two free pairs contribute at most 4 choices.
Union over at most 8 backward clocks yields (1).

The argument never treats an odd center as an ordinary mirrored pair,
and never assumes that a prefix path has a valid finite center. The latter
omission only enlarges the count and is safe for this upper bound.

3. Why even a universal local contraction would not prove existence
------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose, hypothetically, every relevant inverse fiber of G at width ell
had at most C*rho^(ell/2) elements, for a fixed 1<=rho<2. A seed set at
width L contains order 19^(L/2) pair-sum states. The bad language at width
M contains order 10^(M/2) states. At time t, the established width bounds
allow M=L+t/2+O(1). Naively multiplying the inverse bounds through t levels
therefore produces the following schematic exponent in its upper bound:

  (L/2)*(log(10)-log(19)+t*log(rho))
       + (t/4)*log(10) + (t^2/8)*log(rho) + O(t).

This displays the direction of failure: for every rho>1 the quadratic
term grows, and even rho=1 leaves the positive linear target-count term.
More precisely this is the exponent produced by inserting the available
upper width bounds into the elementary product bound; it is NOT a lower
bound on the actual number of hits. Making rho slightly smaller than 2
cannot turn this argument into a summable first-hit estimate.

The needed estimate is about PALINDROMES INSIDE THE ACTUAL IMAGE SUPPORT,
not all palindromes at the same output width. For a finite seed set E,
write H_t={n in E: the first palindrome occurs at time t}. A sufficient
condition is

  sum_{t>=0} |H_t| < |E|.

The H_t are disjoint, but knowing they are disjoint only proves <=|E|.
Any explicit upper bound on |H_t| that tends to zero with t eventually
forces |H_t|=0 by integrality. Thus a successful finite-ensemble hazard
bound must also establish a finite horizon after which no first hit can
occur for that ensemble. Numerical survival alone gives no such horizon.

4. A stronger countermodel: injectivity does not rescue sparse targets
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Consider all nonempty finite decimal strings with nonzero leading digit,
with palindromes as the terminal set. For a nonpalindrome w, write

  w=u a v,

where v is its longest palindromic suffix and a is the preceding digit.
Define F(w)=u a v a: append that digit a. If w is already a palindrome,
stop. (One may define F(w)=w0 on palindromes to make a total function.)

Every step increases the width by one. Each target has at most ONE
predecessor, since removing its final digit uniquely determines the only
possible input. Consequently every iterate also has fibers of size at
most one, much stronger than any proposed exponential contraction.

Nevertheless EVERY finite input reaches a palindrome. If w has length L
and longest palindromic suffix length s, its unpaired prefix has length
D=L-s. The appended word has palindromic suffix a v a, so its new unpaired
prefix length is at most (L+1)-(s+2)=D-1. Initially D<=L-1. Hence stopping
occurs within L-1 steps and at width at most 2L-1.

At width M, palindromes are still exponentially sparse: there are exactly
9*10^(ceil(M/2)-1) among 9*10^(M-1) canonical strings. Width growth, sparse
palindrome targets, and even globally injective iterates coexist with an
empty nonterminating complement. This countermodel is not reverse-and-add;
it isolates why a counting argument needs more than those properties.

5. Quantifiers and remaining positive target
--------------------------------------------
A growing sequence of finite widths L(T), each with a seed surviving T
steps, does not supply one finite seed surviving every T. Compactness can
produce an infinite digit object, which is outside the finite-integer
existence claim. Every fixed palindrome has a finite backward tree because
reverse-and-add strictly increases positive integer values; the countable
union of those finite trees can still cover all positive integers.

A valid positive route would be one fixed finite-width seed ensemble with
a uniform positive survivor count, or a finite/all-length structural
certificate that directly excludes every first hit. The new three-layer
contraction is a genuine correlation estimate, but it does not furnish
either object. No decimal Lychrel witness or all-time avoidance theorem
has been obtained here.

6. Exploratory variable-rho search (not a completed theorem)
-----------------------------------------------------------
I also allowed the shared rho value to vary from pair to pair in {1,...,8}.
The finite search tracks collections of determinized inverse states so
that both bit choices must remain viable. Starting every such test only
from the outer boundary would be insufficient for an exponential rate
bound: a finite prefix can discard a few bit strings and still enter a
full-rate recurrent component later. The corrected search starts from
every reachable nonempty determinized inverse state.

For incoming clocks 001 and 010 this corrected search completed, with
441 and 2,303 collection states respectively and no recurrent surviving
component. Other schedules reached explicit state caps. In particular
clock 000 reached 250,002 collection states after processing 63,927 of
them. Clocks 100 and 110 each reached a 30,001-state cap while constructing
the first determinized inverse automaton. These are UNKNOWN, not exclusions.
No claim about all variable-rho fibers follows.

All exploration processes have finished. The certificate in sections 1-2
is separate from these capped exploratory searches.
