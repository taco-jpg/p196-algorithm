UNIFORM DIGIT-SUM ESCAPE IN REVERSE-AND-ADD ORBITS
14 October 2026

NEW UNIFORM THEOREM

Fix an integer base b >= 2 and a digit-sum threshold B >= 1. There is an
explicit waiting-time bound that works for every positive starting integer,
independently of its digit length and of the starting time.

Write T_b(n) = n + reverse_b(n), S_b(n) for the base-b digit sum, and
kappa_b = 1 + ceil(log_2 b). Define, for positive integers M,

  E_b(1) = 1,
  E_b(M) = kappa_b * [2*(M-1)*E_b(M-1) + 2*M - 1]  for M >= 2.

For every n > 0 and s >= 0, some j satisfies

  0 <= j <= E_b(2*B) and S_b(T_b^(s+j)(n)) > B.

Iteration continues after palindromes. Thus every block of E_b(2*B)+1
consecutive times contains a digit sum exceeding B. Those times have lower
Banach density at least 1/(E_b(2*B)+1). A coarse closed upper bound is
E_b(M) <= (4*kappa_b)^(M-1) * M!.

In decimal kappa_10=5: E(1)=1, E(2)=25, E(3)=525, and E(4)=15785.
These are upper bounds, not optimal escape times.

The proof extends the carry dynamics to finite nonsymmetric profiles,
retaining the forward terminal digit. A sufficiently long zero gap makes
the left component autonomous for a prescribed time; the right component
remains nonzero. Induction on the permitted total mass rules out long gaps
in a bounded-mass prefix. If all gaps are short, the width is bounded,
contradicting the outer-column growth clock. This gives the recurrence.

The complete derivation is in the manuscript and strengthening_notes.txt.
uniform_effective_audit.txt independently checks the explicit induction
and exact coupling. uniform_escape_audit.txt gives a separate compactness
proof of a uniform bound. The new uniform theorem is NOT yet formalized
in Lean. These are AI-assisted internal audits, not human peer review.

Independent integer controls passed 50,972 one-step coupling cases,
31,076 multistep separation checks, and 5,118 support-law cases. Finite
controls check implementations and do not replace the mathematical proof.

RESULT AND LIMIT

Every positive decimal reverse-and-add orbit has unbounded digit sum on
every tail. This is proved for the ordinary integer operation, with iteration
continuing after palindromes, and is fully formalized in Lean 4.19.0.

Writing T(n) = n + reverse_10(n) and S(n) for the ordinary decimal digit sum:

  For every n > 0, B >= 0, and start >= 0, there exists t >= start
  such that S(T^t(n)) > B.

This includes n = 196. It does not prove that 196 avoids palindromes, that
any finite decimal Lychrel number exists, or that none exists. It also does
not prove that the individual digit sums converge to infinity. The result
is unboundedness on every tail, or equivalently infinite limsup.

The requested decimal existence conclusion remains unproved. Publication
novelty and priority for the partial results have not been established.

WHY THE THEOREM HOLDS

Let d_i be the digits of n, indexed from the units position, and let
q_i = d_i + d_(L-1-i). This symmetric column word has positive endpoints
and total mass 2*S(n). Ordinary addition gives forward and backward carry
equations, with carries in {0,1}.

If both corresponding output digits vanish on an interval of length at
least two, the old column word is all zero or all nine on that interval.
For an overflowing step, a long new zero run comes either from an old
run of nines, or from an old zero run that is one position longer. The
boundary extensions are proved explicitly; gaps cannot merge arbitrarily.

Consequently, a uniform digit-sum bound controls both the number of
nonzero columns and the maximum length of every zero gap. It would bound
the digit length of the entire orbit. But every positive reverse-and-add
step strictly increases the integer, so digit length cannot stay bounded.
Applying this argument to each later starting state proves the tail claim.

The eight-page UnboundedDigitSums.pdf contains the complete written proof,
under the title "Uniform digit-sum escape in reverse-and-add orbits."
Its LaTeX source is included. The paper also proves the general-base
unboundedness theorem and a square-root running-maximum bound. In decimal,
if D_N is the largest digit sum at times 0,...,N, it proves

  liminf_(N -> infinity) D_N/sqrt(N) >= 3/4.

The general-base and sharp-constant results have written proofs and a
separate audit in general_base_audit.txt; they are not Lean theorems.
Neither this bound nor uniform escape proves pointwise digit-sum divergence.

CONSEQUENCES FOR THE EXISTENCE SEARCH

1. The fixed-distance moving-wall proposal is now ruled out for every
   finite C. Its all-time embedding conditions would force constant digit
   sum 9*C, contradicting the theorem. The earlier wall identities remain
   valid; the missing all-time finite embedding cannot be supplied.

2. A positive digit-sum-preserving step cannot end at a palindrome.
   The scalar criterion is formalized: if T(n) is a palindrome, either
   S(T(n)) = 2*S(n), or 11*S(T(n)) = 4*S(n). This useful one-step
   condition cannot hold as constant-sum preservation forever.

3. Exact safe renewal families can increase the wall parameter:
   C = 4*m + 2 becomes 6*m + 3 for every m >= 1. This is an all-parameter
   construction, not only a finite search. However, the renewed family
   survives eight further ordered updates and then fails the required
   balance on the ninth. A symbolic proof covers every parameter. These
   renewal results are written and independently checked, not Lean proofs.

4. Every positive decimal integer with digit sum nine reaches a palindrome
   within three additions; 180 -> 261 -> 423 -> 747 attains the bound.
   This has a written proof and finite implementation controls, not Lean.

5. No nonempty universally forward-closed safe family described solely by
   exact length and digit sum exists, including time-indexed families of
   such classes. This concerns universal closure of those classes, not an
   individual orbit or arbitrary digit-dependent invariants. The proof and
   its independent audit are in digit_mass_audit_notes.md.

Finite checks support the implementations but are not proofs of infinite
behavior. The sparse-orbit controls covered 221,869 transitions; a separate
zero-gap audit covered 199,210 cases. These suites overlap and should not
be added as a count of distinct cases. Bounded solver records distinguish
UNSAT from UNKNOWN. None is reported as an all-time exclusion unless an
explicit mathematical argument supplies that conclusion.
