PUBLICATION PRIOR-ART ADDENDUM
Follow-up checked 14 October 2026

Scope
-----
Target: the claim that every positive reverse-and-add orbit in each fixed
base b >= 2, continued after any palindromes, has unbounded digit sum;
the symmetric-column zero-gap estimate used to prove it; and width growth.
This supplements digit_sum_prior_art_notes.txt without replacing it.
No author was contacted, access purchased, or manuscript submitted.

Outcome
-------
The complete Harborth 1973 paper was newly obtained and read. Its growing
block families do not provide bounded-digit-sum counterexamples. The final
2001 Prosper--Veigneau paper, Trigg 1967/1972, and Yamashita 1997 remain
full-text gaps. Their bibliographic records were checked more closely.
The HAL record for Prosper--Veigneau is a bibliographic notice, not a
full-text deposit. The relocated Japanese society archive supplies
Yamashita's issue contents, not his article.

This is a limited source review. It neither establishes priority for the
project's theorem nor establishes the absence of an earlier proof. The
unread papers must not be described as having been checked for all relevant
theorems. In particular, an abstract or a later citation is not a substitute
for comparing the final paper's statements.

1. Harborth 1973: full-text gap closed
-----------------------------------
Heiko Harborth, "On Palindromes," Mathematics Magazine 46, no. 2
(March--April 1973), 96--99.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/0025570X.1973.11976286
JSTOR DOI: https://doi.org/10.2307/2689040
JSTOR record: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2689040

Public author-upload record:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323194244_On_Palindromes
This identifies Heiko Harborth as the uploader, dated 8 March 2022.
Complete public PDF:
https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Heiko-Harborth/publication/323194244_On_Palindromes/links/62276071a39db062db8a4dc0/On-Palindromes.pdf

Obtained: five scanned PDF pages, consisting of a reprint cover and printed
pages 96--99. Read the complete OCR; visually checked the formulas and
theorem on printed pages 97--98.

Evidence/comparison: page 96 defines an infinite increasing sequence.
Page 97 constructs a family in base g = 2^alpha with block period
2 alpha + 2: each complete period increases the zero block and the
(g-1) block by one digit, hence width by two. Page 98 extends the
construction to infinitely many pairwise disjoint palindrome-free orbits.
These are explicit-family results, not a universal width or digit-sum
theorem. The growing nonzero blocks give growing digit mass, rather than a
bounded-mass counterexample. The full paper contains no maximum-zero-run
inequality of the project's kind. Its concluding question concerns eventual
block periodicity in power-of-two bases, not digit-sum divergence.

universal digit-sum theorem or the project's dynamical zero-gap bound was
found in that preprint. Changes between it and the final paper remain
unverified. Section 2 also uses a stop-at-palindrome convention.

5. Styer attribution anchor: exact citation and inspected passages
----------------------------------------------------------------
Robert Styer, "The Palindromic Conjecture and The Fibonacci Sequence,"
Villanova University, author manuscript, revised September 1986, 11 pages.
Title, author, affiliation, and revision date verified visually on page 1.
No journal publication or DOI was established.
https://www41.homepage.villanova.edu/robert.styer/PalindromePaper1986.pdf

The previous pass obtained and read the full manuscript. Section 2, page 3,
uses symmetric remainder/shift data: R_i = q_i mod B and
S_i = floor(q_i/B). Section 4, page 6, bounds the number of successive
pairs at a fixed digit length by 2 + [log_2 B], establishing an elementary
width-growth precedent. Section 5, page 8, Lemmas 2--3 concern the carry
structure when the sum is a palindrome. In particular, a column with
R_k = S_k = 0 separates inner/outer palindrome-output conditions.
This is a static classification of one-step palindrome production, not
the project's bound on the longest zero interval under every transition.
The universal width lemma and symmetric-column representation should not
be introduced as new discoveries.

6. Manuscript framing supported by this review
---------------------------------------------
Use a self-contained proof and make no priority claim. Attribute the
established symmetric-column representation, carry classifications, and
width-growth observations. Explain exactly what is proved here: a bound
on the next maximum zero-run length in terms of the previous maximum and
current digit mass, leading to unbounded digit mass along every positive
orbit. This description distinguishes the project's argument without
claiming that no earlier author had it.

Do not strengthen limsup S_b(n_t) = infinity to a pointwise limit merely
because the classical constructed families have increasing nonzero blocks.
No counterexample to that stronger limit was established by this review,
and its literature status was not settled. A fixed number of digit blocks
is compatible with unbounded digit sum. Width growth alone also does not
imply digit-sum growth.

The remaining concrete priority-check gaps are the final 2001 article,
the two Trigg Mathematics Magazine papers, and Yamashita's five pages.
They can be listed as unread, not as negative search evidence.
