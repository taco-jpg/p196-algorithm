UNIFORM ESCAPE FROM BOUNDED DIGIT SUM: A STRONGER THEOREM

Status: complete mathematical proof below. The earlier zero-run lemmas
and actual-orbit embedding are reused, not reproved in Lean here.
Independent audits of the half-line mechanism, compactness argument, and
explicit coupling/induction have passed; the new theorem is not yet claimed
as part of the previously checked Lean development.
No claim of pointwise digit-sum divergence, or of decimal Lychrel existence,
is made. No novelty claim is made.

1. Main new conclusion
----------------------
For each integer B>=1 there is a finite number F(B), independent of the
starting positive decimal integer n and its width, such that

    some 0<=j<=F(B) satisfies S(T^j(n))>B.

In particular this applies starting at every iterate: a block of F(B)+1
consecutive times can never have all its digit sums <=B. This strengthens
unboundedness on every tail, which by itself gives no uniform waiting time.

An explicit, intentionally very coarse bound is supplied below:

    F(B) = E_10(2B),
    E_10(1)=1,
    E_10(M)=5[2(M-1)E_10(M-1)+2M-1]     (M>=2).       (1)

For example E_10(2)=25, E_10(3)=525, E_10(4)=15785.
These are upper bounds, not claimed optimal escape times.

The proof works in every integer base b>=2. Put

    kappa_b = 1 + ceil(log_2 b).

Then the same statement holds with

    E_b(1)=1,
    E_b(M)=kappa_b[2(M-1)E_b(M-1)+2M-1].             (2)

The argument is elementary and gives a finite recursion. A shorter
compactness proof is described in Section 7, but is not needed for (1).

2. Extend pair-sum dynamics to arbitrary finite half-line profiles
----------------------------------------------------------------
A profile is a sequence q_i, i>=0, with

    0<=q_i<=2b-2,   q_0>0,   finite support.

It need not be symmetric. Write M(q)=sum_i q_i and R(q)=max{i:q_i>0}.
All tails below are exactly zero, not approximate limits.

Define forward carries/digits by

    a_0=0,
    q_i+a_i = r_i + b a_(i+1),     0<=r_i<b.

Define backward carries/digits by scanning from the zero tail:

    q_i+c_(i+1) = u_i + b c_i,     0<=u_i<b,
    c_i=0 sufficiently far to the right.

All carries are 0 or 1. Put h=c_0. Define the new profile Phi(q) by

    h=0:  Phi(q)_i = r_i+u_i                    (all i>=0);

    h=1:  Phi(q)_0 = r_0+1,
          Phi(q)_i = r_i+u_(i-1)                (i>=1).       (3)

The forward digit r_(R+1), if nonzero, is included. Backward carry c_0=1
is precisely the extra digit represented by the '+1' in the h=1 boundary.
There is no deletion of either carry and no loss of mass at infinity.

The output again has finite support and entries between 0 and 2b-2.
For h=1 the exceptional outer entry is at most b, and b<=2b-2 for b>=2.
The outer entry is positive: for h=1 it is r_0+1; for h=0 it is
2q_0+c_1, since h=0 implies q_0<b and the first forward carry is zero.

If q is the genuine finite symmetric pair-sum word of a positive integer,
extended by zero outside its original width, (3) is EXACTLY its next
pair-sum word, also extended by zero. Symmetry identifies backward carries
with reflected forward carries, and h is the actual overflow. In that
case M(q)=2S(n). Thus a theorem for Phi includes actual reverse-and-add.

3. Two previously proved mechanisms survive without symmetry
-----------------------------------------------------------
First, the simultaneous-zero lemma uses only the two carry equations,
not symmetry. An interval of >=2 positions with r_i=u_i=0 must have q
identically 0 or identically b-1. Consequently, writing Z(q) for the
largest INTERNAL zero run between its positive end entries, the exact
old gap inequalities remain valid:

    h=0: Z(Phi(q)) <= max(Z(q),1,floor(M(q)/(b-1))),

    h=1: Z(Phi(q)) <=
          max(max(Z(q)-1,0),2,floor(M(q)/(b-1))+1).             (4)

Trailing infinite zeros are not included in Z. There is one boundary
point to check beyond the original finite symmetric proof: if h=0 and
the right support endpoint grows from R to R+1, any zero interval still
lies within [0,R], and simultaneous-zero classification applies there.
If h=1, the new endpoint is R+1, so a zero run [A,B] of length>=3 has
1<=A<=B<=R and its old extended interval [A-1,B] is in range. Thus no
artificial infinite tail is counted as an inherited internal gap.

Second, support cannot retreat. If h=0, either a_(R+1)=1 and r_(R+1)=1,
or a_(R+1)=0 and r_R=q_R+a_R>0. Hence R(Phi(q))>=R(q).
If h=1,

    Phi(q)_(R+1) = a_(R+1)+u_R > 0.

Indeed, if a_(R+1)=0 then 1<=q_R<=b-1 and u_R=q_R>0. No position beyond
R+1 can be nonzero. Therefore

    R(Phi(q)) = R(q)+1                         when h=1.     (5)

At a non-overflow step the outer coefficient at least doubles:

    q'_0 = 2q_0+c_1 >= 2q_0.

Since 1<=q_0<=2b-2, kappa_b consecutive non-overflow steps are impossible:
they would give q_0>=2^kappa_b>=2b>2b-2. Every block of kappa_b steps
therefore contains an overflow. If g_t counts overflows before time t,

    g_t >= floor(t/kappa_b),
    R(q_t) >= R(q_0)+g_t.                                  (6)

In particular, if mass<=M and all internal zero gaps<=K, then

    R(q_t)+1 <= H:=M+(M-1)K.                               (7)

Equations (6)-(7) force an escape by time kappa_b H, because at that time
g_t>=H, whereas R(q_0)>=0 and (7) requires R(q_t)<=H-1.

4. Exact isolation across a long zero gap
----------------------------------------
Suppose a profile splits as q=p+v, where

    p_0>0,    max supp(p)=A,
    min supp(v)=D>A,    v is nonzero,
    gap length g=D-A-1>=2.

Both p and v have finite support. The zero gap resets both directional
carries. Forward normalization and backward normalization therefore split
as the sums of the separate normalizations of p and v. The global
backward boundary carry h equals that of p.

For the right component, with h fixed by p, define the bulk image

    Psi_h(v)_i = r_i(v)+u_(i-h)(v),

where u_j(v)=0 for j<0 (the support of v is away from the boundary).
There is no extra boundary digit for this component. Then, exactly,

    Phi(p+v) = Phi(p) + Psi_h(v).                          (8)

Reason that the carries split: a forward carry from p can occupy only
the first zero after A and is reset there; backward carry from v can
occupy only the first zero before D and is reset there. Neither crosses
the gap. The supports of the two same-direction digit normalizations
are disjoint. Formula (8) follows by adding these exact digit identities.

The right endpoint of the left component is at most A+1 after a step,
and the left endpoint of the right component is at least D-1. These are
the only support-motion bounds needed: r(p) is supported at indices <=A+1,
u(p) at indices <=A, r(v) at indices >=D, and u(v) at indices >=D-1.
In particular the remaining gap after s steps is at least g-2s while the
components are separated. The right component stays
nonzero: its forward normalized digit sequence represents the positive
integer sum_i v_i b^i, and Psi_h(v) is coefficientwise at least this
nonzero sequence. There is no cancellation.

Consequently, if g>=2T+2, the two components remain separated by >=2 zero
positions through time T. At every time 0<=s<=T:

* the left component evolves autonomously by Phi;
* the right component is nonzero and has mass at least one;
* the total mass is exactly the sum of the component masses;
* the h used in the full profile and in the left profile is identical.

This remains true if v itself has several separated components. Only
its nonzero mass and its distance from the left component are needed.

5. Explicit uniform escape: induction on mass
--------------------------------------------
Claim: every profile with initial mass<=M has an iterate of mass>M by
time E_b(M) from (2). Equivalently, no profile has all masses <=M at
times 0,...,E_b(M).

Base M=1. The only possible profile is q_0=1 and q_i=0 for i>0. Its next
profile has mass two, so E_b(1)=1 works in every base.

Inductive step M>=2. Set T=E_b(M-1), K=2T+1, and

    H=M+(M-1)K = 2(M-1)T+2M-1.

Suppose for contradiction that the whole prefix through E_b(M)=kappa_b H
has mass<=M. This horizon is at least T.

If the initial profile has a zero gap of length >=2T+2, split at that gap.
By Section 4, the left component evolves independently through time T,
and the other component has mass>=1 at all these times. Thus the left
component has mass<=M-1 throughout 0,...,T, contrary to the induction
hypothesis.

Otherwise every initial zero gap has length <=K. Note that T>=M-1
(immediate by induction from (2)), and hence

    K=2T+1 >= 2M-1 >= M+1 >= floor(M/(b-1))+1,
    K>=2.

The gap inequalities (4) therefore keep every gap <=K throughout the
assumed bounded-mass prefix. Its support width is at most H by (7).
But at time kappa_b H, (6) puts its right endpoint at least H, contradicting
that width bound. This completes the induction and proves (2).

A simple closed upper bound, if desired, is

    E_b(M) <= (4 kappa_b)^(M-1) M! .                       (9)

This follows by induction: substituting the preceding bound into (2),
the main term uses only 2 kappa_b (M-1) times that bound, while the target
allows 4 kappa_b M times it; the remaining positive margin exceeds
kappa_b(2M-1). The recursive bound is substantially better than (9).

6. Consequences and limits
--------------------------
Embedding actual pair-sum profiles gives the uniform digit-sum theorem
with F_b(B)=E_b(2B). The bound is independent of n, its initial width,
and all initial zero-gap lengths. It is very coarse; no optimality claim
is intended.

Let A_B={t>=0:S(T^t(n))>B}. Every interval of F_b(B)+1 consecutive integer
times meets A_B. In particular its lower asymptotic density is at least

    1/(F_b(B)+1).

The same bound holds for the lower density uniformly over translated
long time intervals (lower Banach density), by partitioning into blocks
of this fixed length.

This does NOT show S(T^t(n))->infinity. It prevents arbitrarily long runs
below any fixed threshold; it does not prevent infinitely many isolated
returns below that threshold. Nor does the very small density bound prove
that time-averaged digit sums diverge.

7. Independent compactness formulation
--------------------------------------
For a fixed M, let X_M be the set of all profiles on N with alphabet
{0,...,2b-2}, q_0>0, and sum_i q_i<=M. It is closed in a product of finite
discrete spaces: the mass condition is the intersection of conditions on
finite partial sums. Hence X_M is compact. Every member has finite
support because every positive entry contributes at least one to mass.

Phi is continuous on X_M. Forward carries at a fixed output coordinate
depend on a finite prefix. Backward carry at a position depends only on
the first subsequent coefficient different from b-1: coefficients <=b-2
reset it to zero, and coefficients >=b reset it to one. A run of b-1
has length at most floor(M/(b-1)), uniformly on X_M. Thus there is uniformly
finite lookahead for every output coordinate, including the h branch.

The closed subsets of X_M whose first k iterates all have mass<=M are
nested. If they were nonempty for every k, compactness would give an
infinite bounded-mass Phi orbit. But Section 3 and the old gap argument
rule that out: fixed initial Z and bounded mass bound support width,
while (6) forces its right endpoint to infinity. Thus one finite escape
horizon exists uniformly on X_M. Section 5 gives an explicit one.

8. What obstructs stronger conclusions from the old static bound alone
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The preceding running-maximum sqrt(t) estimate used only bounded support
count and a largest-gap bound. Those two static conditions alone cannot
give a linear mass-versus-width bound: a symmetric word with O(M) positive
unit coefficients separated by O(M)-long zero runs has mass O(M) and
width Theta(M^2). These states do not automatically have long bounded-
mass histories. Section 5 is precisely a new historical restriction.

Large one-step collapses also remain possible. For even k>=2, take the
admissible symmetric word

    q=(10, 9 repeated k times, 10).

Its ordinary carried digits, from low to high, are k+1 zeros followed
by 1,1. Thus the reverse-and-add output is

    11 * 10^(k+1),

of digit sum two, whereas the input digit sum is (9k+20)/2. Every such q
has a canonical decimal realization. This rules out any general lower
bound on the next digit sum proportional to the previous digit sum or
current width. It is not a counterexample to divergence along one fixed
orbit.

No O(B)-duration bound, linear running-maximum growth theorem, or
pointwise digit-sum divergence theorem is established here. The explicit
uniform escape and positive lower-density conclusions are the new proved
strengthenings.
