INDEPENDENT AUDIT: EXPLICIT UNIFORM DIGIT-SUM ESCAPE

Audited source: continuation/strengthening_notes.txt, especially Sections
2--5. This is a mathematical audit, not a new Lean formalization. The
conclusion below does not depend on the accompanying finite controls.

VERDICT

The proposed induction and exact recurrence are valid, including for
nonsymmetric finite profiles. No counterexample or missing hypothesis was
found. The distinction between Phi on the anchored left component and
Psi_h on the detached right component is essential and is handled
correctly. Forward terminal carries must be retained, as the source does.

For a base b>=2, write kappa=1+ceil(log_2 b). Define

    E(1)=1,
    E(M)=kappa[2(M-1)E(M-1)+2M-1]  for M>=2.

Every finite profile q on the nonnegative integers, with q_0>0 and
0<=q_i<=2b-2, has some iterate of mass greater than M by time E(M),
provided its initial mass is at most M. If initial mass is already greater
than M, time zero gives the same escape conclusion without a hypothesis.

For ordinary decimal reverse-and-add this gives the width-independent
digit-sum escape horizon F(B)=E_10(2B), B>=1, where kappa_10=5. The claim
does not assert pointwise divergence of the digit sums.

1. PROFILE MAP AND BOUNDARY CHECKS

Extend every finite profile by zero on its entire right tail. Forward
normalization uses a_0=0 and

    q_i+a_i=r_i+b a_(i+1).

Backward normalization is determined uniquely by zero carries on the
right tail and

    q_i+c_(i+1)=u_i+b c_i.

The carry bounds 0<=a_i,c_i<=1 follow from the coefficient bound 2b-2.
Let h=c_0. The map is

    Phi(q)_i=r_i+u_i                         if h=0,
    Phi(q)_0=r_0+1,
    Phi(q)_i=r_i+u_(i-1) for i>=1            if h=1.

All digits r_i are included, in particular the forward terminal digit
r_(R+1)=a_(R+1), where R=max supp(q). Digits u_j at negative indices are
defined as zero only when they appear in the detached-component formula.

The map preserves finite support and the coefficient bound. Ordinary
coordinates are sums of two digits, at most 2b-2. The exceptional outer
coordinate for h=1 is at most b, and b<=2b-2 including base two. Positivity
at zero follows from r_0+1 when h=1. When h=0, q_0+c_1<b, so q_0<b and

    Phi(q)_0=2q_0+c_1>0.

The map therefore remains in the claimed anchored profile class.

2. EXACT SPLITTING ACROSS A ZERO GAP

Let q=p+v, p_0>0, A=max supp(p), D=min supp(v), and g=D-A-1. Here v is
nonzero and lies wholly to the right of p. The required initial gap is
at least two; the one-step normalization identities already hold when
g>=1.

The precise support bounds for the two separate normalizations are:

    r(p): positions <=A+1,     r(v): positions >=D,
    u(p): positions <=A,       u(v): positions >=D-1.

The forward carry from p can create a digit at A+1, but the next incoming
carry a_(A+2)(p) is zero. The backward carry from v can create a digit at
D-1, but c_(D-1)(v)=0; the outgoing backward carry is reset at that zero
column. No carry crosses the separating gap.

When g>=1, the two r supports are disjoint, and the two u supports are
disjoint. Adding each pair of separate carry equations therefore gives
valid digits in {0,...,b-1} and binary carries, with the correct boundary
conditions. Uniqueness of normalization gives the exact identities

    r(p+v)=r(p)+r(v),
    u(p+v)=u(p)+u(v),
    c_0(p+v)=c_0(p).

In particular, the global h is the h of p, not the h that v might have
after translating its own left support endpoint to zero. This resolves
the coupling issue.

With h fixed by p, put

    Psi_h(v)_i=r_i(v)+u_(i-h)(v),   u_j(v)=0 for j<0.

There is no added boundary digit in Psi_h. Directly substituting the
normalization identities proves

    Phi(p+v)=Phi(p)+Psi_h(v).

For h=1, the single boundary '+1' belongs to Phi(p) alone. The right
component has r_0(v)=0, so the equality also holds at coordinate zero.
For h=0, it holds there by the same digit identities.

The supports of Phi(p) and Psi_h(v) need not remain disjoint for an
arbitrarily short gap. The induction only uses gaps large enough to
preserve separation. The exact front bounds needed are

    max supp(Phi(p)) <= A+1,
    min supp(Psi_h(v)) >= D-1.

The second inequality can be sharpened to D-1+h, but that is unnecessary.
These inequalities imply that the new separating gap is at least g-2
whenever the components remain separated.

The right component cannot vanish: forward normalization preserves the
positive integer value sum_i v_i b^i, provided its terminal carry digit
is included. Thus r(v) is nonzero. Since Psi_h(v)>=r(v) coefficientwise,
Psi_h(v) is nonzero and has integer mass at least one. Its coefficients
remain at most 2b-2 because they are sums of two normalized digits.

3. ITERATED COUPLING THROUGH THE INDUCTION HORIZON

Set p_(s+1)=Phi(p_s) and
v_(s+1)=Psi_(h(p_s))(v_s). If the initial gap satisfies g>=2T+2, an
induction on s gives all of the following through times 0,...,T:

    q_s=p_s+v_s;
    the gap is at least g-2s>=2;
    p_s is an anchored valid profile;
    v_s is a valid nonzero finite profile away from the boundary;
    the global h equals h(p_s).

The equality of total masses follows from coefficientwise addition, and
the components are in fact disjoint throughout the required interval.
Consequently a global mass bound M gives mass(p_s)<=M-1 at every one of
these times. Applying the induction hypothesis to p_0 is legitimate:
p_0 itself has initial mass at most M-1, positive outer entry, and the
same coefficient bound. No symmetry or autonomous evolution of v_s is
required.

4. GAP PROPAGATION AND SUPPORT CLOCK WITHOUT SYMMETRY

The simultaneous-zero argument needs only the two carry equations and
binary carry bounds. It remains valid for these independently defined
forward and backward normalizations. A simultaneous-zero interval of
length at least two is an old interval consisting entirely of zeroes or
entirely of b-1.

For h=1, a new zero interval of length k>=3 has an overlapping old core
of length k-1. In the zero-core case, the endpoint carry equations extend
it to an old zero interval of length k+1. In the other case its mass is
at least (b-1)(k-1). This yields exactly the quoted gap inequalities.

The finite right boundary causes no additional zero intervals. Support
never retreats and grows by at most one. For h=0 a terminal forward
carry gives a nonzero digit at R+1; otherwise r_R=q_R+a_R>0. For h=1,

    Phi(q)_(R+1)=a_(R+1)+u_R>0.

If a_(R+1)=0 then 1<=q_R<=b-1 and u_R=q_R>0; otherwise the first term
is already positive. Thus h=1 increases R by exactly one. Every new
internal zero run is contained in indices at most R, where all the old
column equations apply. Infinite trailing zeroes are never counted.

At every h=0 step the outer coefficient at least doubles. Since
2^kappa>=2b>2b-2, kappa consecutive h=0 steps are impossible. Hence each
block of kappa transitions contains an h=1 transition, and

    R(q_t)>=R(q_0)+floor(t/kappa).

If mass is at most M and all internal zero gaps are at most K, there are
at most M positive coefficients and at most M-1 intervening gaps. Thus

    R(q_t)+1 <= M+(M-1)K = H.

At time kappa H the preceding clock gives R(q_t)>=H, contradicting this
width bound. Both the time index and the '+1' in the width are consistent.

5. RECURRENCE AND ALL EDGE CASES

For M=1, positivity at coordinate zero forces the sole profile q_0=1,
q_i=0 for i>0. Its next profile has mass two in every base b>=2, so
E(1)=1 is valid.

For M>=2 put T=E(M-1) and K=2T+1. The recursion gives T>=M-1 by a
straight induction, and its new horizon kappa H is at least T. Moreover,

    K>=2M-1>=M+1>=floor(M/(b-1))+1,
    K>=2.

Suppose every mass at times 0,...,E(M) is at most M. If an initial
internal gap has length at least 2T+2, Section 3 supplies an autonomous
left orbit whose masses remain at most M-1 through time T, contradicting
the induction hypothesis.

Otherwise the initial maximum gap is at most K. The mass ceiling and the
gap inequalities keep it at most K throughout the finite prefix. Section
4 then gives the contradiction at time kappa H. Finally,

    H=M+(M-1)(2T+1)=2(M-1)T+2M-1,

which is exactly the proposed recurrence. The decimal examples E(2)=25,
E(3)=525, and E(4)=15785 are arithmetically correct.

The optional closed upper bound E(M)<=(4kappa)^(M-1) M! also follows
directly. For M>=2 let A=(4kappa)^(M-2)(M-1)!>=1. The induction hypothesis
gives E(M)<=kappa[2(M-1)A+2M-1], which is at most 4kappa M A because
2M-1<=(2M+2)A. The case M=1 is equality.

6. TRANSFER AND STATUS

For a finite symmetric decimal pair-sum profile, reflection identifies
c_i with the corresponding reflected forward carry, h is the actual
overflow, and Phi is the exact next pair-sum word extended by zero.
Its mass is twice the ordinary digit sum. Therefore E_10(2B) applies to
the actual positive integer orbit independently of the starting width.

This audit establishes the mathematical explicit-recursion argument.
It does not promote it to a checked Lean theorem: the previously compiled
Lean theorem DecimalQuantitativeMass.digitSum_escape has a different bound that
still depends on initial decimal length. The new uniform theorem and its
explicit recursion remain paper results unless separately formalized.
