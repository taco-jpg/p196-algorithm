Aperiodic boundary / pushdown continuation

Status: no invariant, finite Lychrel witness, or proof of decimal existence.
No novelty claim is made. These are reproducible exploratory results.

1. A conditioned spatial lift

The companion variable-encoding computation establishes that any infinite
trajectory permanently following the maximal outer clock 8,17 must eventually
follow this eight-phase clock in its first three mirrored digit sums:

(17,13,14), (8,12,9), (17,5,0), (8,13,5),
(17,6,11), (8,14,8), (17,8,17), (8,16,16).

To investigate an aperiodic boundary instead of a fixed-period family,
append one new symbol x=0,...,18 to each phase. The next prefix is computed
exactly with either possible carry entering from the omitted interior.
The resulting 152-state lift has one cyclic strongly connected component
of 65 states, 14 of which branch within that component.

Thus the next spatial layer is NOT forced into a unique doubled period.
A specific substitution is not established by this graph. Temporal choices
remain, and their physical realization depends on the omitted interior.

2. The stronger, constructive universal-carry test

A nonempty set of canonical prefixes closed for BOTH possible interior
carries would give a genuine finite-prefix invariant. One could choose
any compatible finite center, because the orbit's actual carry is among
the two permitted choices; lengths never decrease.

The greatest such set under the prescribed clock is empty at each tested
width. The complete state counts and existential recurrent component sizes:

width     states       universal trap       cyclic SCC size
4            152             0                   65
5          2,888             0                  388
6         54,872             0                2,191

The universal computation repeatedly deletes any state with even one
successor outside the remaining set. This differs decisively from merely
finding a cyclic SCC: an existential SCC allows the interior carry to be
chosen independently at each time, without constructing a single number
that supplies those choices.

These empty traps rule out only this finite-prefix certificate at the
listed widths. They do not rule out larger prefix invariants, correlated
interior carry histories, pushdown invariants, or decimal Lychrel numbers.

3. Why an apparent binary tape does not automatically work

At phase (17,13,14), the recurrent fourth-symbol values include x and x+9
for x=4,...,7. Their possible outward return traces agree. It is tempting
to treat this duplicated low/high sheet as a stored bit.

But suppose an overflow step has no input q-symbol equal to 9. Define
r_i=q_i-9*[q_i>=10]. Every nonneutral symbol fixes its outgoing carry,
and exact mirrored-column addition gives

    q'_0=q'_L=q_0-9,
    q'_i=r_i+r_{i-1}       (1<=i<L).

Consequently, changing any interior symbol x to x+9 for 1<=x<=8
leaves the entire next q-word unchanged, provided the no-9 condition
holds. Ordinary low/high sheet bits disappear after one overflow.
They are not a persistent binary counter.

A neutral 9-run is the possible exception. Carries propagate through it.
For a run entered by carry a from one end and b from the other, its
interior pair sums after the step are 18-9(a+b), either 18,9,or0.
The run remains neutral exactly when a and b are opposite. Thus a
memory-bearing substitution would have to account explicitly for these
neutral runs and their boundary phases. No finite center generating such
a substitution indefinitely was obtained.

4. Finite-center probes

Enumerating every finite center and suffix under the three-pair clock
shows that long transients are possible, but all tested seeds eventually
leave the outer clock. The number of tested words and maximum durations:

pairs     words tested       maximum outer-clock transitions
3                   88                         57
4                1,672                         76
5               31,768                        138
6              603,592                        148

An example attaining 148 is the decimal representative 1500069947897,
with folded pairs (8,14,8,7,4,15) and center pair sum18. Leaving the
maximal clock does not imply reaching a palindrome. These are finite
clock-duration results only, not palindrome-delay records or proofs.
