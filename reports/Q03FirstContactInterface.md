# Q03: the exact first-contact interface in decimal reverse-and-add

**PAPER PROOF.** This is a necessary-condition theorem, not a Lychrel certificate. It extends the previously published gamma=0 four-gate theorem (Agent5Gamma0FirstDefectGate) to the overflow branch and strengthens the premise from equality of complete next pairwords to equality **only through the first-contact column**. The enumeration is independently validated by ordinary decimal integers.

## Exact universal theorem

Let P,R be distinct palindromic pair-coefficient words of the same length n, with entries in [0,18], arising from positive canonical base-10 numbers. Suppose their respective normalizations have the same final overflow g in {0,1}. Let 1<=j<(n-1)/2 be the first lower-half source position at which P and R differ: q=P[j], r=R[j], Delta=r-q, and j'=n-1-j. Assume the normalized successor **pairwords agree for all indices 0..j**, with no requirement on any later index.

Let c_i and d_i be the two incoming ordinary schoolbook carries, let k_i=d_i-c_i, let cL=c_j, cU=c_j', dU=d_j', and let a=P[j-1]=P[j'+1]. All carry bits are physically correlated (0 or 1), not independent port guesses.

For g=0 there are EXACTLY four directed (q,r,cL,cU,dU) gates:
(4,9,1,0,0), (9,4,1,0,0), (9,14,0,1,1), (14,9,0,1,1).

For g=1 there are EXACTLY twenty directed q->r gates, necessarily **r=q+9** for q=0..9 or **r=q-9** for q=9..18; additionally a != 9. The complete carry-port restrictions (an unspecified bit may be either 0 or 1) are:

| q -> r | cL | cU | dU |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 -> 9 | 1 | any | 1 |
| 1..8 -> 10..17, respectively | any | any | any |
| 9 -> 18 | 0 | 0 | any |
| 9 -> 0 | 1 | 1 | any |
| 10..17 -> 1..8, respectively | any | any | any |
| 18 -> 9 | any | any | 0 |

Each of the **24** directed types is physically realizable as a first-contact prefix-preserving one-site mirrored perturbation of a genuine positive decimal integer. In the overflow case all twenty have witnesses for equality of the **entire** next pairword (not a recurrent certificate). The precise witnesses, including actual ordinary-integer additions, are generated by verify_interface.py.

## Proof (universal, no width bound)

Every normalized digit difference is
  (R_i-P_i)+k_i-10 k_(i+1)
with each k_i in {-1,0,1}. For i<j, the inputs and low carries coincide. For g=0, equality at pair position j-1 forces k_(j'+1)=10 k_(j'+2)=0 (both bits are zero); equality at position j then gives
  2 Delta+k_(j')-10 k_(j+1)=0.
Parity forces k_(j')=0, so Delta=5k_(j+1) and an exhaustive two-bit threshold check yields precisely the four rows above.

For g=1, equality at position j-1 forces k_(j'+2)=k_(j'+3)=0. Equality at position j gives
  Delta=10 k_(j+1)-k_(j'+1).
The monotonicity of the threshold floor((x+c)/10) implies both carry differences have the sign of Delta (or are zero). Positive Delta can only be 9 or 10, negative only -9 or -10; Delta=+10/-10 forces nonzero upper carry difference, contradicting the equation. Hence Delta=+9/-9 and k_(j'+1)=+1/-1. Since k_(j'+2)=0 at the unchanged outer coefficient P[j'+1]=a, a cannot equal 9: at coefficient 9 unequal incoming carries would remain unequal on exit. Substituting q,r into the two threshold comparisons enumerates exactly the displayed six grouped cases, with 20 directed q/r possibilities.

This is a necessary prefix gate, not a sufficient condition that the *whole* next pairword or a temporal cycle agrees.
