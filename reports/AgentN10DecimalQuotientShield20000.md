## Construction
Coefficient words are least-significant first. Let `B` be the 39 coefficients and `W` the 20 coefficients in `calculation/new_structures/agent-N10/phase.json`. These are the already known decimal p8 rec0 exterior and lattice, from `calculation/decimal_frontier/lattice_formats/p8_scc.txt`; their discovery is **not** claimed here.
For integer `k>=1` and an even-length palindromic central coefficient word `C`, form
`P(k,C) = B ++ W^k ++ C ++ reverse(W^k) ++ reverse(B)`.
Realize each coefficient `p` (0..18) as decimal digit pair `(floor(p/2), p-floor(p/2))`. This determines an ordinary positive integer with no leading zero. The tested core options are empty, `[0,18,18,0]`, `[2,4,4,2]`, `[9,6,6,9]`.

## Quotient and positive mechanism
The proposed quotient records only the temporal exterior phase `t mod 8` and its certified matching prefix length `h_t`; the remaining palindromic pairword core is an **unrestricted** lift, not a periodically closing centre. At each tested step the integer's pairword begins `B_(t mod 8) ++ W_(t mod 8)^r` and ends in its reflection for `r=floor((h_t-39)/20)` intact periods. The period-eight quotient is stable while the physical integer grows one digit at phases 0,2,4,6. The core grows into the shield: this is finite-horizon reservoir shielding, **not** self-renewal.

For every phase, the 39-column prefix contains an odd coefficient (odd counts respectively 24,19,16,20,16,9,16,14), hence any integer realizing the quotient is nonpalindromic: a palindrome has coefficient pairs `2d_i`, all even. The Python coefficient normalizer reconstructs all eight phases and independently checks a second temporal lap and 15 complete spatial bulk periods per phase.

## Sound guarded-prefix quotient lemma (PAPER PROOF, any base b>=2)
Let `P,Q` be palindromic length-n pair-coefficient words (entries 0..2b-2) agreeing on the first `h>=2` entries, with their common `P[h-1]!=b-1`. Write `T(P)` for the pairword obtained after base-b carry-normalization (allow an extra leading digit). Then both normalizations have identical overflow and `T(P),T(Q)` agree in their first `h-1` coefficients. Proof: low digits through index h-1 agree because the carry starts at zero. Palindromicity makes the final h coefficients agree as well. The carry map `c -> floor((p+c)/b)` on c in {0,1} is constant whenever p differs from b-1. Thus at high index n-h the two carries become equal, and all high digits at indices >=n-h+1 agree; the final overflow agrees too. Pairing high and low output digits, with either overflow, gives equality in the first h-1 pair coefficients. If `P[h-1]=b-1`, shrink h to the preceding shared non-neutral coefficient. This is a sound, computable one-step quotient operation. It proves a finite shielding rule but does not itself prove indefinite closure.
