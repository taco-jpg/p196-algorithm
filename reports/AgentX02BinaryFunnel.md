# Agent X02: a nonperiodic-input / uniform-output carry synchronizer

**Status:** PAPER PROOF for the two-step identity for every h; COMPUTED for the 20,000-step relay. No Lean proof and no infinite decimal Lychrel certificate.

## Construction and exact proof

Let 5 <= a <= 8 and h >= 1. For any bits e_1,...,e_(h-1), define the *least-significant-first pairword* P of length 2h to be the mirror of its half
A=(a+9, a+9 e_1, ..., a+9 e_(h-1)).
Each pair coefficient can be represented as a sum of two decimal digits; choose its high digit min(9,P_j) and low digit P_j-min(9,P_j). The resulting N0 has leading digit 9 and last digit a, so it is a positive nonpalindrome. There are 2^(h-1) distinct N0.

Reverse-and-add of N0 equals decimal normalization of P. Since its first/highest coefficients equal a+9 >= 14, there is an overflow gamma=1. Every coefficient a+9 e (e=0 or 1) is NOT the neutral carry value 9: the outgoing carry is exactly e, regardless of the incoming carry. In the new odd-length word N1, at each interior mirrored position j>=1, overflow shifts the right partner by one column. Its two normalized digits are
u_j = a + e_(j-1) - e_j,
v_(j-1) = a + e_j - e_(j-1).
Their sum is *always* 2a, independent of all bits. The outer pair is a and the new middle pair is 2a. Hence the FULL new pairword is
P(N1) = [a] ++ [2a]^(2h-1) ++ [a].

Therefore the *integer* N2=N1+reverse(N1) equals val_10(P(N1)), independently of all bits. Because 10 <= 2a <= 16, ordinary decimal carry normalization gives, with decimal concatenation notation,
N2=(a+1)(2a-9)^(2h-2)(2a-10)a.
The leading and last decimal digits of N1 are 1 and a-1 (different for a>=5); those of N2 are a+1 and a (different). Thus N0,N1,N2 are all nonpalindromic, and the 2^(h-1) starting choices coalesce at step two. No claim about later iterations follows from this finite identity alone.

## The non-neutral synchronizer is Mealy-machine compatible

On input alphabet {a,a+9}, the one-bit local carry state is *reset* at every column to the input e, and the overflow-paired digit output is 2a. A two-state Mealy decoder therefore consumes an arbitrarily long binary band and erases every bit after one overflow step. This differs from merely postulating a periodic centre: the input bits can be arbitrary and need not be generated by any bounded-period spatial machine. For a=8 the uniform output band follows the six-step bulk cycle 16,14,10,2,4,8,16,... . The open requirement for a certificate is an exact interface that regenerates a suitable binary input band and closes the temporal boundary conditions; erasure alone is not self-renewal.
