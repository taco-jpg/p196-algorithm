## New format and exact soundness statement

For a base `b>=2`, write a number's **palindromic pair word** `P=(P_0,...,P_{n-1})`, where `P_i=d_i+d_{n-1-i}` and `0<=P_i<=2b-2`, least-significant-first. Encode `P` by the lower-half word `a=P[:ceil(n/2)]`, and its length parity `e=n mod 2`. For odd `n`, the centre coefficient `a[-1]` must be even. Every physical positive number has such a word. If one coefficient of `P` is odd, its number is necessarily nonpalindromic.

The **regular-centre certificate** comprises a finite phase cycle `t mod q` and deterministic finite automata `A[t,e]` over alphabet `0..2b-2`, accepting halfwords of arbitrary length. Every accepted physical halfword must have `a[0]>0` and an odd coefficient. If the seed's halfword is accepted at phase zero and for every `t,e,gamma` the exact finite-state relation `R[b,e,gamma]` satisfies `R(A[t,e]) subset A[t+1, (e+gamma) mod 2]`, then the seed is a base-b Lychrel number. This is a **PAPER PROOF** by induction on time: `R` exactly computes the successor pairword, all accepted states retain an odd pair coefficient, and no palindrome can have an odd pair coefficient. The closure inclusions reduce to absence of an accepting path in the finite product of source DFA, exact carry transducer, and complemented target DFA. This is not bounded enumeration and permits unbounded, nonperiodic centre languages.

**Two-sided transducer derivation.** At symmetric position `j` let `a_j=P_j=P_{n-1-j}`; let `L_j=c_j` and `H_j=c_{n-j}` be carries at the two ends, `c_0=0` and `c_n=gamma`. Guess `H_{j+1} in {0,1}`, constrained by `H_j=floor((a_j+H_{j+1})/b)`. Then `L_{j+1}=floor((a_j+L_j)/b)`, `u_j=(a_j+L_j) mod b`, `v_j=(a_j+H_{j+1}) mod b`. For `gamma=0`, the successor halfword emits `u_j+v_j` per pair. For `gamma=1`, it emits `u_0+1` at the outside and `u_j+v_{j-1}` for `j>=1`; the previously emitted high digit `v_{j-1}` is a **single-digit delay register**. For even `n`, accept iff `L_h=H_h`, appending `2v_{h-1}` if `gamma=1`. For odd `n`, the final single centre `z` must be even, with `H_h=floor((z+L_h)/b)`; append `2((z+L_h) mod b)` if `gamma=0`, or `((z+L_h) mod b)+v_{h-1}` if `gamma=1` (with the obvious one-digit boundary adjustment). The guessed path is unique after central consistency, since it is the ordinary deterministic carry chain viewed from both ends. This proves exactness for **every** valid word and every base, independent of tested lengths.

**Growing-centre recurrence.** Denoting halfword length `h_t=ceil(n_t/2)`, parity `e_t=n_t mod2`, and overflow `gamma_t`, the exact recurrence is `n_{t+1}=n_t+gamma_t`, `e_{t+1}=(e_t+gamma_t) mod2`, and `h_{t+1}=h_t+gamma_t*(1-e_t)`. Thus a time-periodic overflow schedule can force unbounded expansion without any fixed central block or spatial-periodic digits. For the decimal p8 overflow `10101010`, there are two added halfword positions per period when starting from even length. DFA-labelled centre languages allow these added positions to carry nonperiodic patterns.
