# Three-step coalescence from connected bulk defects in the decimal period-12 lattice

**PAPER PROOF** for an unbounded family of explicit positive decimal integers, with independently reproduced finite controls. Not a base-10 non-palindromic infinite orbit, a periodic kink, a self-similar centre, or a certificate. The theorem concerns a pair of *different input integers*, not an equality of their first reverse-and-add integer outputs.

## Setting and exact construction

Read `calculation/decimal_frontier/lattice_kinks/two_step_connected/lattice_input.txt`: base 10, temporal period 12, exterior width 34, spatial band period 9, overflow schedule `100010001000`. At time phase `t` and length `n`, define a symmetric coefficient word `P` of length `n` by taking the first 34 lower-half coefficients from the stored exterior row, then `W_t[(i-34) mod 9]` until the centre, and reflecting. For odd `n`, choose the single centre coefficient even, by adding 1 if odd and below 18, otherwise subtracting 1. This is an admissible palindromic pairword (coefficients 0..18 and even at a singleton centre). Its first coefficient is positive.

The 15 certified triples `(t,r,[v0,v1,v2])` are exactly those in `motifs.json`: five each for `t=3,7,11`. Fix any one triple and a nonnegative integer `k`, set `j=52+r+9k`, and choose an integer length `n` with `j+30 < floor(n/2)` (and large enough for the external block). Form `Q` by replacing `P_j,P_(j+1),P_(j+2)` and their three reflected mates with `v0,v1,v2`. Each replacement differs from its baseline coefficient; no other coefficient changes. The resulting coefficient word remains palindromic and admissible, with the same leading coefficient and singleton centre (when odd).

Realize each pairword as an actual positive *canonical* decimal integer by splitting each reflected pair coefficient `q` into a lower digit `q-u` and an upper digit `u=max(q-9,1 if i=0 else 0)`, and at an odd singleton centre put `q/2`. Both integers have exact length `n`, since their first/last digits are positive. Let `R(x)=x+rev(x)`. Then **for all the specified n and all k** the two positive integers `A` and `B` have unequal pairwords after one step, equal entire pairwords after two steps, and `R^3(A)=R^3(B)` while `A != B`, `R(A) != R(B)`, and `R^2(A) != R^2(B)`. In particular their ordinary-integer trajectories coalesce *first at the third reverse-add step*.

## Soundness of the finite carry-cone reduction

For a symmetric pairword, one reverse-add step is exactly normalization by ascending base-10 carries, followed by summing digits at reflected positions. Define the normalized lower digit `O_up(i)=(P_i+c_i) mod 10`, `c_(i+1)=floor((P_i+c_i)/10)`; and the mirrored upper digit `O_down(i)=(P_i+d_i) mod 10`, with `d_(i-1)=floor((P_i+d_i)/10)` scanning the upper-half coefficients backwards. For a no-overflow step the new paired coefficient at lower position `i` is `O_up(i)+O_down(i)`. For an overflow step it is `O_up(i)+O_down(i-1)` at the corresponding position. These are exact elementary identities for ordinary reverse-and-add and apply without symbolic assumptions about remote cells.

The three starting phases `t=3,7,11` have `gamma_t=0`, and their successor phases `t+1=4,8,0` have `gamma_(t+1)=1`. In the input lattice rows there are **no adjacent 9 coefficients**; a non-9 coefficient forces the outgoing carry independent of the incoming bit, so a carry disturbance outside three changed columns resets within at most two unchanged columns. In the next lattice rows there is **no 9 at all**, so once a changed first-step coefficient vanishes the associated carry effect resets after one unchanged cell. The full row/carry identities are finite and checked in `local_certificate.py` against `lattice_input.txt` for each of the 15 triples, including both possible remote centre carries. The exact local window is `[j-12,j+15]`. The checker demonstrates: (i) the first successor differs somewhere, its differing columns are strictly inside the window, and the lowest difference is not divisible by 10; (ii) outside this window all coefficients and incoming carries agree; (iii) the second successor has zero pairword difference at each interior column using the true overflow-shifted mirror index; (iv) at both window boundaries the carry states agree. This proves full-word agreement after two steps for arbitrary interior placement, not merely agreement of finite prefixes.

Every checked window is unchanged when `j` is translated by 9, because the input pairword rows, the cut carry bits and the next-step bulk rows all have spatial period 9, and neither reflecting centre nor exterior boundary is approached under `j+30 < floor(n/2)`. The reported local checks explicitly cover shifts 0,9,18,45,90; period-9 translation of the exact formulas proves *all* shifts. The parity at the far centre is immaterial after the ascending and reflected carries reset, and the checker tests both possible remote centre carries. The independent positive-integer replay checks even and odd source lengths.

To see why the integers cannot coalesce at step 2, let `i` be the smallest position at which their first successor pairwords differ. In every listed case the integer coefficient difference at `i` is not a multiple of 10, and lower coefficients agree. Therefore the difference of the two second-step integers, which are the weighted base-10 values of those first successor pairwords, is nonzero modulo `10^(i+1)`. Their second-step integers are unequal. But the complete pairwords of the second-step integers agree, so normalizing and adding their reflected decimal digits produces the **same** third-step integer. This makes the claimed coalescence time exact.

## Exact reproduction and independent controls

Run from repository root:

```
python3 calculation/decimal_frontier/lattice_kinks/two_step_connected/run_checks.py
```

`local_certificate.py` verifies the exact 15 motif rows, nine-position translation, both remote upper carry boundary bits and finite two-step carry cones; `independent_bigint.py` is independent of this checker and of the C++ discovery engine. It constructs 750 concrete canonical integer pairs at lengths 200,201,218,219,400,401,418,419,800,801,1800,1801 with shifts 0,9,18,36,72 where the margin holds; at every time it cross-checks direct Python big-integer reverse-and-add, independent digit-list schoolbook addition and coefficient-word normalization, including exact equality at step 3 and inequality at steps 0,1,2.

`discover.cpp` independently exhausts exactly **1,000,188** connected all-changed perturbations for lower-half widths 1,2,3, all 9 gamma-zero temporal phases and 9 spatial residues, source lengths `n=400,401`, with every replacement coefficient in 0..18 other than the original. It yields 30 cases, exactly the 15 motifs each at both lengths, and no width 1 or 2 hits. This exhaustive bounded-width scan is a *control* for the positive all-length theorem, not a claim excluding wider patterns. The runner builds C++17 in a temporary directory and compares all hits with the committed `motifs.json`, in addition to all independently checked positive integer cases. All necessary reproducibility files are co-committed; no uncommitted sandbox dependencies, third-party Python modules, or generated files are required.

Precise limitations: the result is for the stated three phases and 15 local coefficient motifs with the specified margins, not all temporal phases, not all possible connected perturbations, not an infinite non-palindromic orbit, not a stationary centre, and not a decimal Lychrel number.
